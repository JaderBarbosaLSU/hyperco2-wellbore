# HEM Survives Scrutiny as the Starting Point

**Bottom line: the Homogeneous Equilibrium Model is a defensible — indeed, the literature-optimal — first extension of the existing single-phase solver, but only with three explicit caveats attached.** The quantitative case is strong: fitted CO₂ phase-change relaxation times are **0.04–2.82 ms** against a wellbore blowdown time scale of minutes (equilibrium Damköhler number ≳10⁵), the only controlled vertical two-phase CO₂ dataset found near-zero slip at 6.5 MPa, and SINTEF's own six-equation two-fluid model "did not clearly outperform the HEM" against pipe-depressurization data. The caveats: (i) HEM systematically underpredicts choked discharge through short restrictions by 0–26% — which is why the choke boundary, not the wellbore interior, should carry the non-equilibrium correction; (ii) the HEM mixture sound speed is discontinuous at the saturation boundary, the principal numerical hazard for the HLLC solver; and (iii) the no-slip assumption fails precisely in the late-blowdown, low-rate tail, where condensate fallback in a 17 cm bore at an area ratio of ~200:1 to the 12.7 mm choke is governed by drift velocities of order 0.1–1 m/s that exceed the mixture velocity. The staged path HEM → HRM → drift-flux is mathematically protected by the subcharacteristic condition (each added equilibrium assumption lowers the sound speed, so the equilibrium limit is a well-behaved limit of the relaxation system), and the whole architecture — MUSCL-Hancock/HLLC, Span–Wagner tabulated closure, conjugate radial heat transfer, quasi-steady choke boundary — survives the extension essentially intact. The deepest finding of this review is negative and valuable: **no vertical CO₂ blowdown dataset, no published CO₂-well DTS phase-transition trace, no measured condensation-side relaxation time, and no transport closure validated above ~10 mm diameter exist** — four publishable gaps the planned PERTT experiment sits directly on.

## 1. The literature splits into pipeline decompression, choke discharge, and a thin wellbore corpus

The transient CO₂ depressurization literature has three centers of gravity — SINTEF/NTNU (pipe depressurization, relaxation-model theory, choked orifice flow), UCL/Mahgerefteh (pipeline rupture, HRM, solids) — and a smaller wellbore corpus (LBNL/T2Well, SINTEF well models, OLGA). The per-study record below carries forward the research notes' verification flags: references marked **[unverified]** were not confirmed against a primary source and must be checked before citation.

### 1.2 Choke/orifice critical flow

**Hammer, Deng, Austegard, Log & Munkejord (2022), *IJMF* 156:104201 — the direct precedent for the planned architecture.** Six pipe blowdowns from ~12 MPa/25 °C through orifices and nozzles of 4.5, 9.0 and **12.7 mm** (the exact target choke size), coupled to a 1-D transient HEM pipe solver (4800 cells, MUSCL+RK, Friedel friction). Findings: **"the choke point is at a non-equilibrium state"**; HEM mass-flux error −26% to 0% (always underpredicting), Henry–Fauske −22% to +5%, delayed-HEM (flashing deferred to the CNT superheat limit) −21% to +10%; against Banasiak–Hafner nozzle data, MARE 7.8% (HEM), 2.5% (D-HEM), 5.4% (HF), with HEM degrading to 17% on the low-pressure subset. Orifice contraction coefficients computed from a Bragg-type force balance: **C_c = 0.75 (12.7 mm), 0.74 (9.0 and 4.5 mm)**, from the incompressible sharp-edge limit π/(π+2) ≈ 0.611; the authors state the orifice value remains uncertain. HEM's choke search "often" locks at the saturation crossing because the sound speed is discontinuous there ([SINTEF record](https://sintef.brage.unit.no/sintef-xmlui/handle/11250/3023978); [preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/outflow_preprint.pdf)).

**Boccardi et al. (ENEA)** — 107 steam-water PSV tests (6 mm orifice, 0.5–1.75 MPa, inlet quality 0.5–10%): HEM never overestimated, underpredicting 2–40%; HNE-DS variance 6.9%; Henry–Fauske mean −0.25% but overestimating up to 27.4% ([CISAP4 paper](https://www.aidic.it/CISAP4/webpapers/33Boccardi.pdf)). **T2Well/ECO2N (Pan, Oldenburg, Wu & Pruess, LBNL, 2011)** — 1-D transient non-isothermal **drift-flux** CO₂–water–NaCl wellbore model coupled to TOUGH2/ECO2N: fully implicit mass/energy (Newton), semi-explicit mixture momentum; Shi et al. **UCL well model (Samuel & Mahgerefteh)** — 1-D HEM, conservative hyperbolic form, Godunov FV, Peng–Robinson EOS, Chen friction, Dittus–Boelter in-well heat transfer, wellhead ghost cells with isentropic/Thompson characteristic relations; **validated against Ketzin single-phase wellbore temperatures** (0.41 kg/s; Möller et al. 2014 data) and benchmarked against OLGA, where PR gave systematically lower temperatures — a cubic-EOS bias argument for Span–Wagner ([UCL PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10080131/3/Samuel%20Ramp-up%20injection%20paper.pdf)). Their Goldeneye ramp-up cases show slower ramps giving *colder* wellhead minima (252/238/226 K for 5 min/30 min/2 h ramps).

**Lindeberg (SINTEF, TCCS-6, 2011)** — quasi-steady marching blowout model of the Sleipner well: rates 181–313 kg/s, outlet temperatures −75 to −97 °C, hydrate zones 200–550 m below wellhead with plug-melt **pulsed release**; dry ice in the well judged unlikely; explicitly called for an instrumented abandoned-well blowout experiment ([TCCS-6 PDF](https://www.sintef.no/globalassets/project/tccs6/presentations/e6/03_co2blowout.pdf)). **Hammer, Deng, Liu, Langsholt & Munkejord (2021, preprint; journal of record unconfirmed)** — the only controlled vertical two-phase CO₂ dataset: IFE FALCON loop, **44 mm vertical pipe at 6.5 MPa**, superficial velocities 0.2–4.0 m/s (gas) / 0.15–3.0 m/s (liquid), holdup by X-ray and gamma densitometry. ## 2. Model comparison: the relaxation hierarchy orders the choices and the data refuse to reward complexity

The mathematically clean frame is Lund's relaxation hierarchy ([Lund, SINTEF PDF](https://www.sintef.no/globalassets/project/co2-dynamics/publications/lund_two-phase_relaxation_hierarchy.pdf); journal metadata SIAM J. Appl. Math. 72(6):1713–1741, 2012 **[from training, unverified]**): a homogeneous (single-velocity) two-phase model with three relaxation processes — volume transfer driven by Δp, heat by ΔT, mass by Δμ — generates eight models; imposing each equilibrium **lowers** the mixture sound speed through sum-of-squares increments (1/ã²_X = 1/ã²_Y + Z ≥ 0), so every equilibrium limit satisfies the **subcharacteristic condition** for any thermodynamically stable EOS. HEM (p–T–μ equilibrium) is the bottom of the hierarchy with the lowest sound speed and is the **only** member whose sound speed is discontinuous at the single-phase boundary. HRM is the hierarchy's pT model with finite μ-relaxation — strictly dissipative relative to HEM and smooth at the dome boundary. Velocity non-equilibrium (drift-flux, two-fluid) sits outside the proven ordering; Lund defers it explicitly.

Against this frame, the empirical record is one-directional. HEM with a reference EOS captures decompression-wave speeds well and bulk two-phase behavior "fairly" but overpredicts the pressure plateau for dense initial states and underpredicts short-restriction discharge; HRM\* fixes the plateau at the cost of a case-dependent relaxation time (0.04–2.82 ms, ~60× spread); the nucleation-based HFM achieves HRM\*-level accuracy (MAPE 6.0% vs 7.6%) with one global parameter set but fitted heterogeneous rates; the six-equation two-fluid model did **not** beat HEM and its own authors recommend HEM ([Munkejord & Hammer 2015 preprint](https://www.sintef.no/globalassets/project/co2-dynamics/publications/depress-mix-preprint.pdf); [HFM preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/hfm_preprint.pdf)). Drift-flux earns its keep only where mixture velocity falls to the drift-velocity scale — O(0.1–1 m/s) per T2Well's Kutateladze closure — and the one vertical CO₂ dataset shows no-slip winning outright at 6.5 MPa ([Hammer et al. 2021 preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/driftflux-well.pdf)). No Saurel-type seven-equation CO₂ decompression validation was located; conditional hyperbolicity of single-pressure six-equation models and the Baer–Nunziato remedy are standard knowledge **[unverified primary]**.

## 3. Verdict: HEM is defensible as the starting point — with the failure modes named and bounded

The working hypothesis survives critical testing on every quantitative axis examined, provided its documented biases are treated as known model-form error rather than discovered later.

**Time scales.** Fitted CO₂ flashing relaxation times are 0.04–2.82 ms ([HFM preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/hfm_preprint.pdf)); the well drains through a 12.7 mm choke over minutes. Taking θ ≤ 3 ms against a transport residence time of 10¹–10² s (mixture velocities of order 1–10 m/s over 610 m), the equilibrium Damköhler number is ≥10⁴–10⁵: local equilibrium is an excellent bulk approximation everywhere except within the first metres behind a fast rarefaction head and inside the choke itself (transit ≤ ~0.25 ms at 50–100 m/s through a plate ≤ one diameter thick — firmly inside Fauske's ~0.1 m non-equilibrium regime, [LANL review](https://public.lanl.gov/davidp/MSThesisHTML/2.html)).

**Metastability.** The documented HEM plateau errors are for dense/liquid inventories. For a gas-phase start, the relevant non-equilibrium is delayed condensation — observed once (droplets at 2.8 MPa vs higher equilibrium dew pressure, Test 3, [Munkejord 2020 preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/depress-co2-exp.pdf)) and bounded by CNT in Hammer et al. (2025), *Chem. Eng. Sci.* 309:121415. Near-critical dome entries show essentially zero undershoot, and the well starts at reduced temperature ≈ 0.99–1.06, so the warm early inventory crosses the dome nearly in equilibrium; supercooling grows as the column cools. Crucially, **no measured condensation-side relaxation time exists** — the 0.04–2.8 ms values are all flashing-side. This is simultaneously the weakest link in the HEM justification and a publishable gap.

**Slip.** At 6.5 MPa in a 44 mm vertical pipe, no-slip beat every drift-flux law tested ([Hammer et al. 2021](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/driftflux-well.pdf)) — but their own caveat, "except at low inlet liquid holdup," flags the regime where the blowdown problem lives. At 1–4 MPa the liquid/vapor density ratio reaches order 10–50; the choked mass flux through 12.7 mm, spread over a bore with ~200× the area, gives in-bore superficial velocities that fall to and below the O(0.3–0.7 m/s) Kutateladze drift velocity. Below the Turner-type critical gas velocity, condensate cannot be lifted: fallback, a liquid sump on the closed bottom, and countercurrent segregation follow — and HEM then mispredicts inventory distribution and the DTS-visible phase-front locations. This is an inference from cited density/geometry facts plus standard flooding physics; no CO₂-well demonstration exists.

**Known mispredictions to expect.** Wave propagation: equilibrium kink too sharp; measured transition smooth, between equilibrium and frozen curves. Quality/dry-out: ~0.5–2 s early in 61.7 m pipes. Temperature: tracks T_sat(p) correctly while two-phase (the saturation clamp is HEM's most reliable output); the large temperature errors observed are heat-transfer-closure failures, not equilibrium failures. Discharge: 0–26% underprediction at short restrictions, which couples back into the pressure history (HEM at the choke overpredicted the pipe plateau in Hammer et al. 2022).

**Transitions and limits.** The single↔two-phase transition is thermodynamically trivial in HEM (the flash decides) but numerically delicate (Section 9). Near-critical: the lever rule degenerates and the flash ill-conditions as ρ′−ρ″→0; published codes handle it by dense tabulation, nothing smarter was found. Triple point (0.518 MPa, 216.59 K): reachable only late and essentially at/downstream of the choke; the pure-CO₂ equilibrium sound speed is **zero at the triple point** ([Munkejord 2021 preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/depress-mix-exp.pdf)), so the wellbore model should not carry a solid phase — a three-phase flash belongs in the choke submodel if at all (Hammer, Ervik & Munkejord 2013, *IEC Res.* 52(29):9965–9978, provides the thermodynamic machinery).

**Global vs local reliability — the honest summary.** HEM is trustworthy for globally conserved and saturation-clamped quantities: pressure histories, inventory, two-phase column temperature, wave arrival to within the smoothing noted. It is untrustworthy for local, closure-sensitive quantities: discharge rate through short restrictions, local quality/void during fast transients, dry-out timing, phase distribution at low rates, and anything downstream of the triple point. The staged plan should be read as: Stage 1 earns the global quantities; the later stages chase the local ones.

## 4. HEM governing equations: the solver changes less than the closures do

In conservative mixture form, with mixture density ρ, velocity u, total energy E = ρ(e + u²/2), well inclination giving gravity component g_z, area A constant:

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} = 0$$

$$\frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u^2 + p)}{\partial x} = -\rho g_z - \frac{f_D}{2D}\rho u |u|$$

$$\frac{\partial E}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial\big[(E + p)u\big]}{\partial x} = -\rho g_z u + \frac{4 q''_w}{D}$$

These are *identical in form* to the existing single-phase system. Under full equilibrium, ρ and e are mixture quantities built from saturated phase states at a common (p, T_sat(p)):

$$\frac{1}{\rho} = v = (1-x)\,v'(T) + x\,v''(T), \qquad e = (1-x)\,e'(T) + x\,e''(T),$$

with quality x the lever-rule coordinate. Unchanged: the finite-volume update, MUSCL-Hancock reconstruction (reconstruct in primitive variables (ρ, u, p) as now), the conjugate completion/formation radial network and its coupling through q″_w, the gravity and geometry treatment, and the fiber-optic output machinery. Changed: (i) the thermodynamic closure (ρ,e)→(p,T,a) must return two-phase states, quality, and the **equilibrium** sound speed a_eq inside the dome (Section 5); (ii) the friction closure needs a two-phase evaluation (Section 7); (iii) the wall heat-transfer coefficient needs regime logic (Section 8); (iv) the choke boundary needs a two-phase critical-flux routine and, per the evidence, a non-equilibrium correction (Section 10). No new PDE, no new wave family: eigenvalues remain u, u ± a with a = a_eq(ρ,e) in the dome. The full-equilibrium sound speed is (Flåtten & Lund, [subchar.pdf](https://www.sintef.no/globalassets/project/co2-dynamics/publications/subchar.pdf)):

$$\frac{1}{a_{\mathrm{HEM}}^2} = \frac{1}{a_p^2} + \rho T\!\left[\frac{\rho_g \alpha_g}{c_{p,g}}\left(\frac{ds_g}{dp}\right)_{\!\mathrm{sat}}^2 + \frac{\rho_l \alpha_l}{c_{p,l}}\left(\frac{ds_l}{dp}\right)_{\!\mathrm{sat}}^2\right], \qquad \frac{1}{a_p^2} = \rho\left(\frac{\alpha_g}{\rho_g c_g^2} + \frac{\alpha_l}{\rho_l c_l^2}\right),$$

where a_p is the Wood (mechanical-equilibrium) speed and the saturation-line entropy derivatives supply the extra compliance from phase change. Note a_HEM ≤ a_Wood ≤ a_frozen — the practical consequence is that the explicit CFL constraint inside the dome is *looser* than in single phase; the time step stays governed by the single-phase region.

## 5. Thermodynamic closure: the (ρ,e) flash reduces to one scalar equation in T

The published pattern (Giljarhus, Munkejord & Skaugen 2012, *IEC Res.* 51(2):1006–1014, the foundational density–energy Span–Wagner method for CO₂ CFD; extended to vapor–liquid–solid in Hammer, Ervik & Munkejord 2013 — [Munkejord publ. list](https://pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/publ.html)) maps directly onto the existing code:

**Two-phase detection.** Given a cell's (ρ, e): compute the saturated bracket energies e′_sat(ρ), e″_sat(ρ) from a precomputed 1-D saturation-line table (T_sat, p_sat, ρ′, ρ″, e′, e″, s′, s″ parameterized by T). If e lies between the brackets (and ρ between ρ″(T_tr) and ρ′ limits), the state is in the dome; otherwise the existing single-phase (ρ,e)→T Newton applies unchanged.

**The reduced flash.** For a pure substance, the general UV-flash is 4 nonlinear equations, but saturation relations collapse it to **one scalar equation in T** ([Kumar et al., arXiv:2408.09164](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09164v2)): with v = 1/ρ fixed,

$$F(T) = \big(1 - x(T)\big)\,e'(T) + x(T)\,e''(T) - e = 0, \qquad x(T) = \frac{v - v'(T)}{v''(T) - v'(T)},$$

solved by Newton with bisection safeguard inside the bracket; then p = p_sat(T), and a = a_eq from the formula above (or the equivalent (∂p/∂ρ)_s with equilibrium-constrained derivatives, as SINTEF does). Kumar et al. report an order-of-magnitude CPU reduction on a tank blowdown and 3–4× on an HLLC pipe case from this reduction alone. The lever rule applies on v and e simultaneously by construction; **quality x (mass fraction) and void fraction α = x v″/v must never be conflated** — α is what gravity head and DTS-visible density respond to, x is what the energy balance transports; at 2 MPa, x = 0.1 corresponds to α ≈ 0.7–0.8.

**Acceleration.** Direct Span–Wagner evaluation can consume ~90% of runtime; SINTEF's (T,p)-indexed look-up tables achieved **100–300× speedups at ~10⁻³ % deviation** ([SINTEF 2007 record](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/1274087)). For the dome, the existing 512² (ln ρ, T) tables are *not* usable, since (ρ,T) is not a complete state pair under saturation. Two published options: (a) **separate 1-D saturation-line splines + reduced flash** — the minimal change, recommended; (b) a full (ρ,e)-space 2-D table spanning the dome — Brown et al. (UCL) built adaptive inverse-interpolation tables on a (ρ,U) grid with **70% of nodes inside the envelope, 15% each hugging the dew and bubble lines**, Akima splines, mean deviations 0.005% (T) and 0.44% (p); a uniform (p,T) grid "failed outright" ([UCL Discovery PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1522588/1/Brown%20et%20al%20Thermodynamic%20interpolation%20for%20the%20simulation%20of%20two-phase%20flow%20of%20non-ideal%20mixtures%20AAM.pdf)). A rumored standalone "SINTEF SW-spline" journal paper **does not verifiably exist** — do not cite one.

**Near-critical behavior.** The lever rule degenerates as ρ′−ρ″ → 0 and the saturation-line Newton ill-conditions; no published special fix was found beyond dense tabulation near T_c — flag this as an implementation risk to be handled by local grid refinement of the saturation table and a fallback to the single-phase branch within a small ε of T_c.

**CoolProp organization (background orientation, not from the research notes — standard knowledge, verify against CoolProp documentation before citing):** CoolProp implements the Span–Wagner Helmholtz formulation with (ρ,T) as native variables, uses ancillary saturation equations as initial guesses for a Newton saturation solve, routes general two-property flashes (including (ρ,e)… via `DmassUmass`) through 1-D/2-D Newton iterations with phase detection against the saturation envelope, and offers tabular backends (TTSE and bicubic) for order-of-magnitude speedups — i.e., the same architecture as options (a)/(b) above, and a useful reference implementation and cross-check for the in-house flash.

## 6. Mixture transport properties: effective properties are regressions, not physics

The critical conceptual distinction, which the report's downstream choices all rest on: a **constitutive property** (μ, k of a single phase) relates local flux to local gradient and is measurable independent of geometry; an **effective mixture property** (μ_m, k_m) is a homogenization artifact defined only relative to the model that consumes it — "whatever makes f(Re_m) reproduce Δp"; a **two-phase multiplier** (φ²_l: Lockhart–Martinelli, Friedel, Müller-Steinhagen–Heck) abandons the effective-fluid fiction and correlates the pressure-gradient ratio directly — empirically superior precisely because it does not pretend a continuum property exists; a **wall-HTC correlation** is a boundary-condition closure, not a property at all. Error chains must not cross categories: a μ_m tuned for friction fixes nothing about Pr_m for heat transfer.

**Viscosity.** Verified forms and years ([Ghajar & Bhagwat 2013 PDF](https://aghajar.okstate.edu/sites/default/files/pub-pdfs/Ghajar%20%26%20Bhagwat_HTE%2034(13)_pp%201044-1059%20(2013).pdf)): McAdams (1942) 1/μ_m = x/μ_g + (1−x)/μ_l; Cicchitti (1960) μ_m = xμ_g + (1−x)μ_l; Dukler (1964) volume-weighted; Beattie & Whalley (1982) Einstein-suspension hybrid on homogeneous void; Awad & Muzychka (2008) Maxwell-bound effective-property forms. Their quantitative verdict on 616 annular points across 9.5–50 mm pipes: the best model (Awad–Muzychka 1) captured only **60.4% of data within ±30%**, Cicchitti 57.6% — and "none … predict accurately for all pipe sizes." The Thermopedia assessment is blunter: the homogeneous model works only at high velocities with strongly interdispersed phases, and "adjusting the viscosity definition alone does not fix this" ([Thermopedia](https://www.thermopedia.com/de/content/37/)). Two mitigating facts for this application: μ_m enters turbulent friction only as μ_m^0.25, so a factor-2 disagreement moves Δp_f by ~19%; and as p → p_c all definitions converge (μ_l/μ_g ~ 3–10 for near-critical CO₂). Recommendation: **McAdams (or Cicchitti) baseline, Beattie–Whalley as the sensitivity alternative, and an explicit statement that at G = 10–100 kg/m²s in a 170 mm bore the gravity head — i.e., the void fraction — dominates the pressure gradient, not friction.** Convenience averages for μ_m are tolerable only because the quantity barely matters; they must not be promoted into k_m or c_p,m by analogy.

**Conductivity.** No verified source endorses an effective two-phase k_m as a wall-transfer property, and verified practice is uniformly against it: the UCL CO₂ blowdown code (Dittus–Boelter + Rohsenow — [UCL MATEC 2018](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10064608/1/matecconf_icchmt2018_01026.pdf)), the Cheng–Ribatski–Thome CO₂ model ([Thermopedia](https://thermopedia.com/content/10372/)), and Shah 2017 all build h on single-phase properties plus two-phase enhancement/suppression. The physical reason: wall transfer is controlled by near-wall structure (liquid film, nucleation, dry patches); the wall sees k_l or k_g, never their average. k_m is legitimate only as a stagnant-dispersion conduction property (Wiener/Maxwell bounds) — relevant solely in the late-blowdown near-stagnant limit — or inside a deliberately crude "effective Dittus–Boelter" sensitivity bracket, whose error no published study has even quantified (a stated negative result).

## 7. Wall friction: homogeneous Darcy–Weisbach baseline, Müller-Steinhagen–Heck as the sensitivity upgrade

The HEM momentum equation's self-consistent closure is homogeneous Darcy–Weisbach with f_D from the existing Churchill correlation evaluated at Re_m = GD/μ_m — retaining the current friction architecture verbatim. The best-supported simple alternative is **Müller-Steinhagen & Heck (1986)**: ranked first (with Grönnerud) on EPFL refrigerant-evaporation data, with Friedel third at ~30% standard deviation single-component ([Ould Didi, Kattan & Thome 2002 record](https://iifiir.org/en/fridoc/prediction-of-two-phase-pressure-gradients-of-refrigerants-in-119317); [Thermopedia](https://www.thermopedia.com/de/content/37/)). Its structure — dp/dx|_f = [A + 2(B−A)x](1−x)^{1/3} + Bx³ with A, B the all-liquid and all-gas single-phase gradients **[formula from training, 1986 paper not fetched]** — is C⁰-continuous in x with exact single-phase limits, which matters for transient stability. The famous databank confirmations (Tribbe & Müller-Steinhagen 2000; Xu & Fang 2012, MAD ≈ 28.8% often quoted) were **not verified this session — do not quote their statistics**.

Honest applicability statement: every databank found is D ≲ 50 mm and G ≳ 100 kg/m²s; **no large-databank friction study at D > 100 mm exists at all** — a genuine literature gap. All steady correlations are applied quasi-steadily per cell per step; the defense is time-scale separation (friction response ~D/u* ≪ blowdown time), which is an assumption, not a result — no source quantifies the quasi-steady error. Recommended posture: homogeneous f(Re_m(μ_McAdams)) baseline; MSH as the sensitivity alternative; report friction-closure uncertainty as subordinate to void-fraction/gravity-head uncertainty, which is structural to HEM.

## 8. Wall heat transfer: regime logic under conjugate coupling, with every correlation an extrapolation in diameter

The conceptual anchor: in blowdown, the latent-heat sink is the **bulk** — depressurization flashes/condenses the mixture and the fluid temperature tracks T_sat(p) regardless of the wall — while the wall HTC sets only the conjugate heat leak from completion and formation into the fluid. Wall **nucleate boiling** is a separate phenomenon that switches on only when T_w − T_sat exceeds the onset superheat. The direct precedent (Teng/UCL, HEM blowdown of a 50 mm steel pipe with lumped conjugate wall) used exactly this logic — Dittus–Boelter single-phase, Rohsenow nucleate boiling two-phase — and produced the application-critical verified finding: **"the rate of CO₂ solid formation in the pipe is limited by heat transfer at the pipe wall,"** with wall heating able to evaporate liquid before the triple point is crossed, and adiabatic treatment overestimating solid mass by 15–50% ([UCL MATEC 2018](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10064608/1/matecconf_icchmt2018_01026.pdf)). For a finite-inventory well with a conjugate network already built, wall heat input sets the duration of the saturation-clamped period — Lindeberg's adiabatic conclusion does not apply.

Correlation inventory vs the application (170 mm, G ~ 10–100 kg/m²s, 220–320 K): the CO₂-specific Cheng–Ribatski–(Wojtan)–Thome model is validated for **D = 0.529–9.52 mm and G ≥ 100 kg/m²s** ([Thermopedia](https://thermopedia.com/content/10372/)) — 18× under the bore diameter with a mass-flux floor at the application's ceiling: formally inapplicable; its portable piece is the reduced-pressure nucleate-boiling scaling. **Shah (2017), *Int. J. Refrig.* 74:604–624** is the least-bad extrapolation: 4852 points, 30 fluids including CO₂, D = 0.38–27.1 mm, G = 15–2437 kg/m²s, reduced pressure to 0.787, **MAD 18.6%** ([IIR record](https://iifiir.org/en/fridoc/unified-correlation-for-heat-transfer-during-boiling-in-plain-140363)) — still a 6× diameter extrapolation. **No CO₂ flow-boiling data above ~10 mm diameter were located; every two-phase wall-HTC evaluation in this well is an extrapolation in D and mostly in G** — frame the HTC as a sensitivity study (±50% blanket), not a validated closure.

Recommended initial implementation, mirroring verified practice and LOCA-code structure (RELAP5 mode-ladder specifics are **[T — manuals located at NRC ADAMS, not fetched]**): (i) single-phase — Dittus–Boelter/Gnielinski with a **natural-convection floor** (Churchill–Chu-type), because h ∝ G^0.8 → 0 unphysically as flow dies while buoyant convection persists in a 170 mm vertical bore — this is precisely the failure the SINTEF N₂ closed-end test exposed (−150 °C predicted vs −78 °C measured); (ii) two-phase with T_w > T_sat — Chen-type superposition or Shah 2017, plus a pool-boiling floor (Rohsenow per the UCL precedent, or Cooper reduced-pressure form **[Cooper 1984 unverified]**); prefer smooth superposition/asymptotic blends (Chen S-factor; Liu–Winterton h² form **[unverified]**) over hard if/else switches, which inject HTC discontinuities into the conjugate wall ODEs; (iii) two-phase with T_w < T_sat (condensation on cold walls, the relevant mode when warm wellbore gas contacts deep cold completions or during late repressurization) — Shah condensation **[1979/2009 years unverified]**; (iv) h = max(forced, natural) within branches for regime continuity. Stated negative result: flashing-specific wall-HTC literature at near-zero wall superheat is essentially absent; the low-velocity bottomhole regime (near-stagnant two-phase natural convection in a large vertical enclosure) has no published closure at all — the weakest closure in the model, by construction.

## 9. Numerical implications: keep HLLC, respect the dome-boundary kink

**Hyperbolicity is not the problem.** The sum-of-squares construction guarantees a real a_eq for any thermodynamically stable EOS, so the HEM system is unconditionally hyperbolic ([Flåtten & Lund, subchar.pdf](https://www.sintef.no/globalassets/project/co2-dynamics/publications/subchar.pdf)). The pathology is loss of *smoothness*: p(ρ,e) has a kink at the dome boundary and the sound speed jumps (CO₂ at 5 MPa, 287.43 K: c_g = 201.5 m/s, c_l = 398.9 m/s single-phase, versus a two-phase equilibrium value of order tens of m/s — the decompression plateau at 6 MPa computes to ~40 m/s; the water analog drops 1543 → ~1 m/s on the liquid side). Only full p–T–μ equilibrium produces the discontinuity — HRM's frozen acoustics are smooth, which is why the HEM→HRM stage *improves numerics as well as physics* ([Lund hierarchy PDF](https://www.sintef.no/globalassets/project/co2-dynamics/publications/lund_two-phase_relaxation_hierarchy.pdf)).

**Expected wave anatomy.** The kink makes p(ρ,e) behave like a Menikoff–Plohr piecewise-smooth EOS: rarefactions crossing the dome **split into composite double fans**, directly observed in CO₂ HEM Test 4 of Log, Munkejord & Hammer (2021), *Computers & Fluids* 227:105023, and captured by their HLLC-type solvers as a kinked fan without special treatment, at locally first-order accuracy ([preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/hllcs-hem.pdf)). Kumar et al. attribute their degraded convergence orders to the discontinuous flash derivatives, not instability ([arXiv:2408.09164](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09164v2)).

**Published HLLC experience for HEM CO₂.** No examined source reports HLLC failing *at the dome boundary* in a straight conservative duct; the documented failures (negative internal energy, spikes) in Log et al. are driven by the non-conservative area-change term and resonance — absent from this constant-area well. Kumar et al. run plain HLLC + forward Euler on CO₂ HEM with no reported oscillations. SINTEF's production choice of the more dissipative FORCE flux at CFL 0.85 for its validation code reads as robustness-first pragmatism, and signals that extra dissipation at mixed faces is an accepted trade. Brown et al. (UCL) ran AUSM+ at CFL 0.2 stably on two-phase shock tubes.

**Recommended strategy** (inference from adjacent evidence; no systematic HLLC-vs-fallback failure statistics exist — stated gap): (1) retain HLLC with Davis/Einfeldt wave-speed estimates, taking at faces that straddle the dome the **maximum of the left/right sound speeds** — conservative by the subcharacteristic ordering, so no equilibrium wave can outrun the estimate; (2) guard positivity by checking intermediate-state density and internal energy and locally falling back to HLL/Rusanov on violation (Log et al.: pressure-based intermediate variables help; negative internal energy was the observed failure mode); (3) flash every cell after each stage with phase detection against the saturation brackets, flooring x ∈ [0,1] and clipping tiny negative vapor masses; (4) accept locally first-order resolution of the dome-crossing kink; refine the grid rather than engineer the flux; (5) note the CFL constraint inside the dome is *looser* (a_HEM < a_single-phase) — the time step remains governed by single-phase regions; the late-blowdown hazards are flash non-convergence at low pressure and strong-rarefaction positivity, handled in published codes by robust flash fallbacks and dissipative fluxes rather than formal positivity-preserving limiters (none of the examined CO₂ papers uses one — stated negative result).

## 10. The choke: equilibrium wellbore plus non-equilibrium restriction is the published, physically justified hybrid

The residence-time split is decisive: the 610 m wellbore is ~6,000 Fauske relaxation lengths (local equilibrium overwhelmingly justified); the ≤12.7 mm-thick plate is a small fraction of one (transit ≤ ~0.25 ms, far below the ~1 ms-scale flashing relaxation) — firmly non-equilibrium. **Hammer et al. (2022) did exactly this**: an equilibrium HEM transient pipe solver with Henry–Fauske and delayed-HEM restriction models as the outflow boundary, finding the hybrid matches CO₂ blowdown data better than HEM-everywhere, with HF best for the pipe cases ([preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/outflow_preprint.pdf)). The coupling pattern is also the current code's: evaluate the quasi-steady restriction model from the boundary-cell state treated as stagnation, area-scale the returned mass flux, impose it at the boundary; declare choking when velocity reaches the local sound speed along the expansion path, else set the flux by back-pressure. Two cautions from the same source: the boundary-cell-as-stagnation approximation neglects the pipe-side velocity head (second-order except late in blowdown), and the HEM G-maximization has a kink at the saturation pressure — **bracket the saturation point explicitly and search the single-phase and two-phase branches separately**, or Newton searches oscillate; HEM "often" chokes exactly at the saturation crossing.

**The C_d tension must be resolved, not papered over.** The physically grounded contraction coefficient for a sharp-edged orifice passing flashing CO₂ is **C_c ≈ 0.74–0.75** (Hammer et al., Bragg-type force balance from the incompressible π/(π+2) ≈ 0.611 limit), while the current C_d = 0.84 sits in the compressible-gas-orifice range. The two errors currently compensate: HEM underpredicts G by ~0–26%, and the high C_d pulls the product back toward the data. This is fortuitous, state-dependent, and must be made explicit: **switching to HF or HNE-DS while keeping C_d = 0.84 would overpredict.** Recommended: for the gas-dominated early blowdown, HEM flux with C_d = 0.84 is defensible; once the choke-inlet state is two-phase or liquid-like, move to (HF or D-HEM) paired with C_c ≈ 0.75, and calibrate the (model, C_d) pair jointly against the facility's own Coriolis/rate data. If the device is a choke bean (L/D ~ 2–6) rather than a thin plate, partial reattachment raises effective C_d toward 0.8–0.9 — geometry must be pinned down before calibration. Interface consistency: the imposed boundary flux must carry mass and total energy (including kinetic and gravitational terms) consistently with the interior conservative update; the hybrid's thermodynamic inconsistency (the choke generates entropy/metastability the wellbore model forbids) is confined to the boundary cell and is the same approximation Hammer et al. accepted — but beware double-counting: if the wellbore HEM delivers a boundary state whose quality is an equilibrium artifact, HF's non-equilibrium correction partially re-corrects vapor that was never really there; this matters most when the flash front sits near the wellhead. Choked/unchoked switching must be continuous: as upstream pressure decays, G_choked must meet the subsonic orifice flux at the matching back-pressure; the HEM sound-speed discontinuity can make this switch non-smooth and needs regularization in code. No formal error analysis of the equilibrium-pipe/non-equilibrium-choke split exists (practiced, not proven — stated gap).

## 11. The staged hierarchy is correctly ordered — with one re-prioritization

The proposed ladder HEM → improved transport closures → HRM → drift-flux/two-fluid is broadly right; the critical assessment changes one thing: **the non-equilibrium choke correction (part of the HEM stage's boundary condition) and the HRM stage outrank transport-closure refinement**, because the documented first-order errors (discharge rate, plateau) are phase-kinetic, while friction/HTC refinements move second-order terms in this G–D regime. The gravity-head/void error, by contrast, is structural and is what ultimately forces the drift-flux stage.

**Level 0 — HEM (implement now).** Added equations: none. Closures: equilibrium flash, McAdams μ_m + Churchill, regime-logic HTC, HEM choke flux with explicit saturation-bracketing. Gains: full two-phase capability, saturation-clamped temperatures, correct inventory bookkeeping. Cost: flash-dominated; mitigate with saturation-line splines. Numerical risk: dome-boundary kink (Section 9). Validation: SINTEF Test 3 + dense tests, Botros curves.

**Level 1 — transport-closure upgrades + non-equilibrium choke (sensitivity).** Added equations: none. Closures: MSH friction, Shah 2017/Chen HTC with floors, HF or D-HEM at the choke with C_c ≈ 0.75. Gains: bounds the closure sensitivity; fixes the one documented first-order HEM bias (discharge). Cost: trivial. Validation: Hammer et al. 2022 orifice data (exact 12.7 mm size), COOLTRANS vents.

**Level 2 — HRM\* (implement when evidence demands).** Added equations: **one** — vapor-mass with Γ = (ρy_eq − ρy)/θ, integrated implicitly (Godunov splitting per Log et al.). Closures: θ, treated as an uncertainty parameter spanning 0.05–3 ms (a single constant does not transfer across initial states — 60× spread; a Downar-Zapolski-form state dependence refit to CO₂ follows the Angielczyk/Haida line, originals **[unverified]**). Properties: metastable single-phase extensions of Span–Wagner. Gains: smooth (frozen) acoustics at the dome — a numerical improvement — plus corrected plateau/undershoot; the honest caveat is that **the condensation-side θ for the gas-phase well is uncharacterized** — the one dataset is Hammer et al. 2025 (CES 309:121415), which should be obtained in full. Cost: ~10%. Validation: Log et al. 2024 initial-state study.

**Level 3 — drift-flux slip (defer until evidence).** Added equations: none to the hyperbolic core if added algebraically in the fluxes (one extra PDE if phasic mass is tracked). Closures: Shi et al. (2005) C₀ and drift velocity as modified by Pan & Oldenburg (Ku = 142, smoothed regime interpolation) — take forms from the SPE original. Gains: condensate fallback, sump formation, countercurrent segregation — the late-blowdown physics HEM structurally cannot represent, and the regime where DTS phase-front locations go wrong. Trigger quantitatively defined in Section 14. Validation: only the FALCON loop (44 mm, 6.5 MPa, wrong regime) exists — this level will be validated by the PERTT experiment itself or not at all.

**Level 4 — two-fluid (not recommended).** +3–4 PDEs, interfacial closures that "would require detailed local experimental data that do not exist" (SINTEF's own words), conditional hyperbolicity management, ~2× cost — against a record in which the six-equation model failed to beat HEM. Defensible only if the experiment produces local void/slip data that drift-flux demonstrably cannot fit.

## 12. Verification, validation, and uncertainty: a composite ladder by necessity

**Numerical verification.** (1) Exact conservation of mass and total energy to round-off over long closed-boundary runs, including dome-crossing cells. (2) Stationarity: a hydrostatic, geothermal two-phase column (lower liquid, upper vapor, interface at T_sat(p)) must remain at rest — the two-phase analog of the current well-balancing test. (3) Single-phase limit: results identical to the existing solver for trajectories that never enter the dome, and continuous as a trajectory grazes the dew line. (4) Saturation-crossing 1-D Riemann problems: Kumar et al.'s 200 m tube (100 bar/300 K ‖ 30 bar/300 K, t = 0.2 s) with published reference profiles, and Log et al. Test 4 exhibiting the diagnostic **split rarefaction** ([arXiv:2408.09164](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09164v2); [Log et al. preprint](https://www.pvv.ntnu.no/~stm/dring/hllcs-hem.pdf)). (5) Two-phase acoustics: small-amplitude waves in a uniform two-phase state propagating at the computed a_HEM. (6) Grid/time convergence: expect second order in smooth regions degrading to first at the dome kink — document, don't fight. (7) Positivity stress tests: strong double-rarefactions into near-vacuum. (8) Edwards–O'Brien horizontal pipe blowdown (1970, J. Br. Nucl. Energy Soc. 9:125–135 **[page range via secondary sources]**) for cross-community credibility — note it is water, so it exercises the flash machinery only if an IAPWS backend is added; optional.

**Validation datasets and what each validates.** *SINTEF ECCSEL* (Munkejord et al. 2020; **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3928227**): Tier-1, open raw data, complete geometry/sensor/uncertainty documentation. **Gas-phase Test 3 (4.04 MPa, 10.2 °C) is the single most important case** — nearly the well's state, exhibiting decompression-induced condensation; validates wave speeds, condensation onset, pressure histories. Dense Tests 4/6/8 validate the two-phase closure and (with the N₂ reference) expose heat-transfer-closure failure modes. *CO₂–N₂/He companion* (2021; **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3984822**) adds mixture robustness if ever needed. *Botros 2016 pure-CO₂ shock tube* (DOI 10.1115/1.4031941): decompression-wave speed vs pressure — the cleanest EOS/characteristics test. *COOLTRANS vertical vents* (12/25/50 mm at 150 barg with Coriolis mass flow): the surface-choke boundary-condition benchmark, including the 50 mm delayed-nucleation excess. *Guo et al. Test 1* (gas, 3.7 MPa, 310.6 K, vertical 15 mm orifice, 258 m): large-scale warm gas-phase cross-check closest to the well. *Hammer et al. 2022 orifice blowdowns*: the choke model at the exact 12.7 mm size. *Vessel blowdowns* (INERIS sphere once retrieved; Gebbeken & Eggers once verified): long-time energy balance, flashing onset, stratification — the analog of the well's slowly draining lower inventory. **No dataset satisfies all of (vertical, >100 m, gas-phase ~5 MPa, choked release, distributed axial P/T): validation is composite by construction, and the PERTT blowdown would be the first dataset to close that gap.**

**Dominant model-form uncertainties per predicted quantity.** Discharge rate: choke non-equilibrium model + C_d (±10–25%, the largest single lever). Pressure history: choke flux (which sets drainage rate) then EOS (negligible with Span–Wagner). Two-phase column temperature: essentially pinned to T_sat(p) — small model-form error; single-phase-region temperature: wall-HTC closure (demonstrated −72 °C-class errors at low velocity in the N₂ test). Phase-front locations/void profile: no-slip assumption (unbounded at low rates — structural). Late-time behavior near the triple point: wall heat input (solid formation is wall-HT-limited per the UCL precedent) and the uncharacterized condensation kinetics. Propagate the choke (C_d, model), θ (0.05–3 ms), HTC (±50%), and μ_m/friction alternates through a sensitivity matrix rather than a single calibrated run.

## 13. DTS interpretation: the saturation thermometer is sound physics and an unclaimed observation

In an equilibrium two-phase column, T = T_sat(p(z)): a DTS trace plus a single pressure gauge pins the pressure profile and locates phase fronts — the "saturation thermometer." The thermodynamic basis is solid and implicitly recognized at Ketzin (whose operators flushed with N₂ precisely because condensation/evaporation rate asymmetry causes pressure problems — [Würdemann et al. 2010](https://gfzpublic.gfz-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_242409_1/component/file_242408/15975.pdf)), **but no published DTS trace of a two-phase CO₂ column in a well exists** — Ketzin's record is single-phase by design, and the geothermal flash-point analog, while standard practice, was not verified to a citable source this session. This is the clearest open observational niche the planned experiment can claim.

Expected signatures, in model-output terms: (i) **flashing/condensation onset** — the depth at which the local T departs from the single-phase expansion-cooling trend and locks onto T_sat(p(z)), a kink that propagates as the dome boundary moves; (ii) **plateaus** — along a two-phase interval the DTS trace follows the saturation curve's gentle hydrostatic slope dT_sat/dz = (dT/dp)_sat · ρ_m g, qualitatively distinct from both the geothermal gradient and the single-phase transient; (iii) **slip/segregation signatures** — HEM predicts a single connected two-phase interval, whereas fallback and sump formation produce a liquid-rich bottom (T at T_sat but with the mixture-density-driven pressure profile of a liquid column) and possibly a dry middle section; a saturation plateau whose implied hydrostatic gradient disagrees with the no-slip ρ_m(z) is direct slip evidence; (iv) **post-shut-in evaporative cooling** at the receding liquid surface. HEM-vs-HRM distinguishability on DTS is marginal (θ ~ ms against DTS integration times of seconds) *except* at condensation onset, where supercooled vapor would show T temporarily **below** T_sat(p) — a few-K, transient signature that is the one field-observable discriminator of condensation non-equilibrium, and precisely the uncharacterized quantity (Section 3). Additional model outputs to emit now, cheaply: T_sat(p(z,t)) alongside T(z,t); the superheat/supercool margin T − T_sat; quality x(z,t) and void α(z,t) separately; the phase-boundary depths vs time; and synthetic DTS traces with the instrument's spatial/temporal averaging applied, so model-data comparison is apples-to-apples.

## 14. Recommendations A–H and the prioritized roadmap

**A. HEM as the start? Yes.** Grounds: Damköhler ≥10⁴ (θ = 0.04–2.8 ms vs minutes), near-zero undershoot for warm dome entries, no-slip winning on the only vertical CO₂ data, two-fluid failing to beat HEM, and architecture preservation. Conditions: non-equilibrium correction at the choke from the outset; dome-boundary numerics per Section 9; the known biases logged as expected model-form error.

**B. Thermodynamic closure.** Span–Wagner (not cubic — the PR-vs-OLGA temperature bias is documented) via the reduced UV-flash: saturation-line 1-D splines + one-scalar Newton in T with lever-rule quality on v and e; keep the existing single-phase tables and Newton untouched outside the dome; consider a Brown-style (ρ,e) dome table (dense dew/bubble-line nodes; never uniform) only if profiling demands it; cross-check against CoolProp.

**C. First transport closures.** McAdams μ_m in Darcy–Weisbach/Churchill (baseline), MSH as sensitivity; regime-logic wall HTC — Dittus–Boelter/Gnielinski + natural-convection floor; Shah 2017 or Chen superposition + pool-boiling floor for boiling walls; Shah condensation for cold walls; smooth blends, no hard switches; no effective k_m anywhere except an acknowledged bracketing exercise; ±50% HTC uncertainty band declared up front.

**D. HLLC retained? Yes.** Max-of-neighbors sound speeds at dome-straddling faces, positivity-checked intermediate states with local HLL/Rusanov fallback, post-stage flash with bracketed phase detection, accepted first-order behavior at the kink. FORCE-style extra dissipation is the documented fallback if robustness problems emerge, at modest accuracy cost for a plateau-dominated problem.

**E. Key HEM limitations (ranked).** (1) Choke discharge underprediction 0–26% — corrected at the boundary, Level 1. (2) No-slip void/gravity-head error at low rates — structural, Level 3. (3) Plateau/undershoot bias from delayed phase change — Level 2. (4) Equilibrium condensation onset (supercooled vapor unrepresented) — uncharacterized, experiment-facing. (5) No solid phase — confine to the choke submodel. (6) Near-critical flash conditioning — implementation care, not physics.

**F. Validation datasets.** SINTEF 2020 Test 3 + N₂ reference + dense tests (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3928227); Botros 2016 (10.1115/1.4031941); Hammer et al. 2022 orifice data; COOLTRANS vents; Guo Test 1; CO₂–N₂/He (10.5281/zenodo.3984822) optional; vessel blowdown for long-time energy balance once Gebbeken & Eggers or the INERIS sphere is verified/retrieved.

**G. Quantitative triggers to move beyond HEM.** → Level 1 choke correction: immediately (evidence already exists). → Level 2 HRM: when choke-inlet states are two-phase *and* measured discharge or near-wellhead pressure undershoot disagrees with HEM beyond the C_d uncertainty (~±10%); or when predicted vapor supercooling (from a passive CNT/supersaturation diagnostic run inside HEM) exceeds a few K over resolvable durations. → Level 3 drift-flux: when the local mixture velocity falls below ~3× the Shi/Kutateladze drift velocity (≈1–2 m/s in-bore) over a persistent two-phase interval — equivalently when the choked flux drops to the point that G·A_bore implies superficial gas velocities under ~1 m/s — or when measured DTS phase-front positions/hydrostatic gradients contradict no-slip reconstruction. → Level 4 two-fluid: only on local void/slip measurements that drift-flux cannot fit.

**H. Publication opportunities (strongest first).** (1) **First vertical, wellbore-scale CO₂ blowdown dataset** — distributed P/T + DTS on a 610 m well; Lindeberg called for exactly this in 2011 and nobody has done it. (2) **First published CO₂-well saturation-thermometer DTS trace** — flashing-onset kinks, saturation plateaus, phase-front migration; a standalone observational paper. (3) **Condensation-side relaxation time for CO₂** — the fitted θ literature is all flashing-side; a gas-phase well blowdown with DTS supercooling signatures could bound it for the first time (complementing Hammer et al. 2025). (4) **Transport-closure performance at 170 mm** — every friction/HTC correlation is extrapolated ≥6× in diameter; even bulk-calorimetric validation at well scale is novel. (5) The model paper itself: HEM + conjugate radial network + non-equilibrium choke, validated on the above — the composite is unpublished territory.

**Roadmap.** *Implement now:* two-phase flash (reduced UV, saturation splines) + dome-aware HLLC guards; McAdams/Churchill friction; regime-logic HTC with floors; choke saturation-bracketing plus HF/D-HEM option with C_c ≈ 0.75 and documented C_d policy; verification suite (conservation, stationarity, single-phase limit, Kumar/Log shock tubes); validation vs SINTEF Test 3 and Botros; emit T_sat, supercool margin, x, α, phase-front depths, synthetic DTS. *Sensitivity study:* MSH vs homogeneous friction; Cicchitti/Beattie–Whalley μ_m; Shah vs Chen+floors HTC ±50%; C_d ∈ [0.74, 0.84] × {HEM, HF, D-HEM}; θ ∈ [0.05, 3] ms via a shadow-HRM run; grid/CFL. *Defer until evidence:* HRM as a production stage (pending condensation-θ evidence); drift-flux slip (pending low-rate trigger or DTS contradiction); two-fluid (pending local data); solid-CO₂/triple-point flash (choke submodel only, pending observed near-triple-point operation); full (ρ,e) dome tables (pending profiling).

## Conclusion

The critical test the hypothesis was set leaves HEM standing for a reason worth internalizing: the physics that HEM gets wrong is concentrated where residence times are short (the choke) and where velocities are low (the late-blowdown tail), and both failure modes can be handled without touching the hyperbolic core — one as a boundary-condition correction with direct 12.7 mm experimental precedent, the other as an algebraic drift-flux closure with a quantitative activation trigger. The deeper strategic finding is that the validation landscape is inverted relative to the usual situation: the model is more constrained than the application. Horizontal pipes, shock tubes and vessels over-determine the EOS, wave dynamics and phase kinetics, while the application's defining features — verticality, 170 mm bore, conjugate wall heating, choked finite-inventory drainage, and the DTS observable — have **zero** published data among them. That makes the planned experiment not a validation exercise for the model but the field's first dataset in four distinct gaps, and it argues for designing the model's outputs (supercool margins, phase-front depths, synthetic DTS) around what the experiment can uniquely measure, rather than around what the pipe-decompression literature already knows.
