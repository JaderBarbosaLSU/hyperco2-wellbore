# Phase 1–2 Results: SCMM Reconstruction and Godunov Comparison

Benchmark: Gessner (2010) single-phase shock tube (air, 500 kPa/400 K vs
100 kPa/300 K, 4 m closed tube), 4001 nodes / 4000 cells (Δx = 1 mm),
output at t = 3 ms, reference = exact Riemann solution. Mean absolute
errors over the full domain. Generated by
`scripts/run_single_phase_benchmark.py`; raw numbers in
`results/single_phase_errors.json`.

## Phase 1 — validation of the Python SCMM reconstruction

| Scheme | P [Pa] | U [m/s] | ρ [kg/m³] | T [K] | Thesis P [Pa] | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-cons., 1st order | 3715 | 5.17 | 0.0301 | 2.39 | 3339 | ~11 % |
| Non-cons., 2nd order | 3027 | 4.29 | 0.0230 | 2.02 | 2713 | ~12 % |
| Conservative, 1st order | **1523** | **1.95** | **0.0159** | **1.71** | 1555 | **~2 %** |
| Conservative, 2nd order | 383 | 0.44 | 0.0041 | 0.35 | 311.6 | ~20 % |

Assessment:

* **Conservative/1st order matches the thesis to ~2 % on every variable** —
  the strongest evidence that the reconstruction is faithful, since this
  variant involves no interpretive choices.
* The non-conservative runs differ by ~11–12 %, consistent with the
  shock-entropy correction algorithm being undocumented in the thesis (our
  implementation tracks the contact surface and imposes the
  Rankine–Hugoniot entropy jump on right-chamber material; Sec. 1.1 of
  `docs/original_formulation.md`). Field plots reproduce the thesis
  phenomenology, including the characteristic shock-position lag.
* Conservative/2nd order is within ~20 %; the error is dominated by the
  dispersive wiggle at the shock, whose amplitude is sensitive to solver
  details (pivoting in the banded solver, Picard iteration counts and
  tolerance sequencing). The qualitative behavior (dispersion, not
  diffusion) matches the thesis exactly.
* The error *hierarchy* of thesis Table 3.3 is reproduced in full:
  cons < non-cons, 2nd < 1st, and the cost ordering too.

## Head-to-head findings

1. **Accuracy per grid point.** First-order Godunov/HLLC (316 Pa) already
   matches the *best* original scheme, SCMM conservative 2nd order (383 Pa,
   thesis 312 Pa). MUSCL-Hancock is ~10× more accurate than both.
2. **Cost.** On identical grids: 1.4–2 s (explicit FV at CFL 0.9) vs
   84–399 s (implicit SCMM with Picard-iterated banded solves at effective
   CFL ≈ 0.6). Factor ≈ 100–300×. The SCMM cost is intrinsic to the
   construction (several banded factorizations of a 3N×3N system per step),
   not a Python artifact — both codes are vectorized NumPy.
3. **Conservation.** FV conserves mass and energy to machine precision by
   construction; SCMM discretizes the quasi-linear form and does not. The
   visible consequence is the shock-position lag of the non-conservative
   SCMM (≈5 cm at 3 ms) and the residual shock-speed error of the
   conservative SCMM.
4. **Oscillation control.** The limiter (absent in SCMM by design) removes
   the 2nd-order dispersive wiggle at the shock while keeping 2nd-order
   accuracy in smooth regions; van Leer slightly beats minmod, as expected.
5. **Exact vs approximate Riemann solver.** HLLC is indistinguishable from
   the exact solver on this problem (315.6 vs 310.7 Pa) — the usual result
   for ideal-gas shock tubes. HLLC also extends naturally to general EOS
   and carries the contact exactly, which matters for the two-phase and
   real-fluid CO₂ stages; HLL smears the contact and is kept only as a
   robust fallback.
6. **What SCMM retains in its favor.** (a) The implicit formulation has no
   CFL stability bound — potentially useful for slow near-wellbore
   transients after the initial blowdown; (b) the primitive/entropy
   formulation couples to an arbitrary EOS through (a, β, cp) without flux
   Jacobians. Both advantages are reproducible within a FV framework
   (implicit FV; primitive-variable reconstruction with a general-EOS
   HLLC), so neither argues for carrying the SCMM architecture forward.

## Recommendation for the wellbore model (to be revisited after the two-phase benchmark)

Adopt the **finite-volume MUSCL-Hancock + HLLC** architecture as the
foundation for Phase 3, with the SCMM solvers retained in the repository as
validated references and regression baselines. Pending confirmation on the
two-phase shock tube (Städtke benchmark, Phase 1 remainder), the same
conclusion is expected to hold there; the two-phase extension will use a
HLLC-type solver on the homogeneous-mixture system with the Wood sound
speed, where the non-conservative α_g advection requires the standard
non-conservative-product treatment.
