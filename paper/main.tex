\begin{abstract}
This report documents, at a level intended to permit independent
reproduction, a one-dimensional transient compressible-flow model for the
blowdown of a pressurized vertical well through a wellhead choke. The
governing mass, momentum, and total-energy equations are solved in
conservative form with a Godunov-type finite-volume method
(MUSCL--Hancock reconstruction, HLLC approximate Riemann solver), an
architecture selected on the evidence of systematic shock-tube benchmarks
against the split-coefficient-matrix method of earlier work
\citep{gessner2010}. Gravity, wall friction, and fluid--wall heat
transfer enter as source terms; the gravitational work term is
discretized in an energy-consistent face-flux form that closes the
discrete total-plus-potential-energy budget to machine precision. The
thermodynamic closure is isolated behind a property interface
implemented both by a calorically perfect gas (nitrogen baseline) and by
the Span--Wagner reference equation of state for \co\ evaluated through
CoolProp, with a tabulated $(\rho,e)\!\to\!(p,T,a)$ inversion whose
interpolation error is quantified. The wellhead boundary condition
couples the interior solution to a quasi-steady choke through the
outgoing characteristic; for \co\ the choke model is extended to a
homogeneous-equilibrium (HEM) formulation that admits flashing within
the restriction while the distributed wellbore flow remains strictly
single phase, monitored every time step against the saturation boundary
and halted with a flagged report on any in-wellbore dome entry. Initial
conditions are hydrostatic and, in the preferred configuration,
geothermal: $T(\zeta,0)=T_s+\Gamma\zeta$ with a Louisiana-summer surface
temperature and a Gulf-Coast gradient, applied consistently to the fluid
column and to the conjugate completion--formation thermal model of the
LSU \co\ research well. Verification spans exact shock-tube solutions,
conservation audits, hydrostatic stationarity, grid/CFL studies, the
lumped-vessel limit, real-fluid property and choke-flow limits, and
independent transient-conduction references, with errors quantified
throughout. Results are reported for matched nitrogen and \co\
blowdowns of the \SI{2000}{ft} well from a \SI{5}{MPa} wellhead state:
\co\ stores $2.56\times$ the nitrogen mass at matched conditions but
discharges only $1.40\times$ faster initially; with the uniform
\SI{300}{K} initial state the choke flashes (HEM throat quality
$0.95$--$1.0$) and the adiabatic wellbore reaches saturation at the
bottom within \SI{173}{s}, whereas with the geothermal summer
initialization and the coupled thermal model the entire system ---
wellbore and choke throat --- remains single phase for the full
one-hour transient with a minimum saturation margin of
\SI{25.6}{\degree F} (\SI{14.2}{K}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%=============================================================

Transient depressurization (blowdown) of wells and pipelines containing
compressed gas is a canonical problem in well control, \co\ transport and
storage operations, and the repurposing of oil and gas wells for energy
storage. The rapid opening of a wellhead valve launches an expansion wave
into the wellbore, followed by a long quasi-static discharge during which
the contained fluid cools substantially by expansion work. For \co, the
associated temperature excursions can cross the saturation locus, with
consequences for well integrity and metering; a predictive transient model
is therefore a prerequisite for the design and interpretation of blowdown
and injection tests at facilities such as the LSU \co\ research well.

The present work is the continuation of a numerical framework whose
starting point was the Fortran implementation of the split coefficient
matrix method (SCMM) developed in \citet{gessner2010} following
\citet{chakravarthy1980} and \citet{stadtke2006}, with linear systems
solved by the banded routine of \citet{aziz1979}. In Phases~1--2 the
original single-phase SCMM solvers were reconstructed in Python and
validated against exact shock-tube solutions, reproducing the original
error tables; Godunov-type finite-volume (FV) alternatives were then
evaluated on identical benchmarks. The FV method with MUSCL--Hancock
reconstruction \citep{vanleer1984,toro2009} and the HLLC approximate
Riemann solver \citep{toro1994} proved approximately one order of
magnitude more accurate than the best original scheme at two orders of
magnitude lower computational cost, with exact discrete conservation ---
properties the quasi-linear SCMM discretization cannot provide. That
evidence, rather than a priori preference, fixed the architecture
adopted here.

This report consolidates the complete technical documentation of the
framework through Phase~5: (i)~the mathematical model, its constitutive
relations and source terms, with the rationale for and limitations of
every closure; (ii)~the real-fluid \co\ thermodynamic treatment;
(iii)~the initial conditions, including hydrostatic initialization
consistent with a geothermal temperature profile; (iv)~every boundary
condition, including the wellhead choke with its homogeneous-equilibrium
(HEM) extension for flashing within the restriction; (v)~the conjugate
completion--formation heat-transfer model with full documentation of the
empirical correlations employed; (vi)~the numerical method; (vii)~the
verification and validation campaign with quantified errors; and
(viii)~results and their physical interpretation for matched nitrogen
and \co\ blowdowns, including the fiber-optic sensor outputs of the LSU
well. A reproducibility section (\cref{sec:repro}) tabulates every
parameter of every reported case. Negative results and model failures
encountered during development are documented explicitly rather than
omitted.

\section{Problem formulation}
%=============================================================
\label{sec:problem}

A vertical well of depth $L = \SI{609.6}{m}$ (\SI{2000}{ft}) and constant
circular cross-section (inner diameter $D$, flow area $A = \pi D^2/4$) is
initially closed and filled with quiescent gas. The geometry is that of
the LSU \co\ research well: only the 7-5/8\,in corrosion-resistant-alloy
(CRA) inner casing contains the pressurized fluid, giving
$D = \SI{170.3}{mm}$ (35.8\,lb/ft API wall). At $t=0$ a choke of bore
diameter $d_c = \SI{12.7}{mm}$ and discharge coefficient $C_d$ at the
wellhead is opened instantaneously to an external environment at back
pressure $p_b = \SI{101.325}{kPa}$.

\Cref{fig:well-schematic} reproduces the facility design schematic of
the well: the vertical and cross-sectional views with the full casing
program, and the inner completion. The complete well extends to a
measured depth of \SI{8409}{ft} with a horizontal section, but a
9-5/8\,in bridge plug at \SI{2000}{ft} isolates the upper vertical
interval that constitutes the present computational domain: the
pressurized fluid occupies the 7-5/8\,in CRA inner casing from the
wellhead to the bridge plug, which is the closed bottom boundary of
\cref{sec:bc-bottom}. The 2-7/8\,in tubing, 1\,in injection line,

\paragraph{Coordinate system and sign conventions.} The axial coordinate
$z$ is measured \emph{upward} from the closed bottom ($z=0$) to the
wellhead ($z=L$); velocity $u$ is positive upward (toward the wellhead).
Depth below the wellhead is denoted $\zeta = L - z$, so that the
geothermal profile of \cref{sec:ic-temperature} reads
$T = T_s + \Gamma\,\zeta$ with $\Gamma > 0$. All spatial results are
reported exclusively in terms of the depth $\zeta$, plotted increasing
downward; the upward coordinate $z$ appears only in the governing
equations.

\paragraph{Units convention.} Results are reported in US customary
units --- psi, \si{\degree F}, ft/s, lbm/s, ft --- as the primary
scales of every figure, with the SI equivalents carried on the
secondary scales (upper abscissae and right ordinates). Temperature
\emph{differences} (saturation margins) convert as
$\SI{1}{K} = \SI{1.8}{\degree F}$. In the text, results quote US
customary first with SI in parentheses; model formulation,
correlations, and verification metrics remain in SI.

Two working fluids are considered on identical geometry, grid, choke,
and output cadence: nitrogen (ideal gas) as the baseline, and \co\ with
the full real-fluid treatment of \cref{sec:co2}. Two initial states are
considered: a hydrostatically initialized isothermal column at
$T_0=\SI{300}{K}$, and the geothermal summer initialization of
\cref{sec:ic-temperature}. The wellhead initial pressure is \SI{5}{MPa}
in the fluid-comparison campaigns --- a deliberate deviation from the
original nitrogen baseline at \SI{13.79}{MPa}, which is retained
unchanged in \cref{sec:results-n2}: at \SI{13.79}{MPa} and \SI{300}{K},
\co\ is a dense liquid-like fluid, not a single-phase gas, so no matched
gaseous comparison exists at that state. The quantities of interest are
the fields $p(z,t)$, $T(z,t)$, $\rho(z,t)$, $u(z,t)$, the wellhead mass
flow rate $\mdot(t)$, the mass inventory $M(t)$, the saturation margin
field $m(z,t) = T - T_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)$ for \co, and the temperature
histories at the fiber-optic sensor locations of the LSU well
(\cref{sec:sensors}).

\section{Mathematical model}
%=============================================================
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Governing equations}

One-dimensional transient compressible flow along the well axis is
governed by the conservation of mass, momentum, and total energy,
\begin{align}
\pd{\rho}{t} + \pd{(\rho u)}{z} &= 0, \label{eq:mass}\\
\pd{(\rho u)}{t} + \pd{}{z}\!\left(\rho u^{2} + p\right) &=
  -\rho g \;-\; \frac{f_D\,\rho\,u\,|u|}{2D}, \label{eq:mom}\\
\pd{E}{t} + \pd{}{z}\!\left[u\left(E + p\right)\right] &=
  -\rho u g \;+\; \frac{4\,q_w''}{D}, \label{eq:energy}
\end{align}
with the conservative variables $\mathbf{V} = [\rho,\;\rho u,\;E]^T$,
the flux vector
$\mathbf{F} = [\rho u,\;\rho u^2 + p,\;u(E+p)]^T$, and the primitive
variables $(\rho, u, p)$. Here $E = \rho\,(e + u^2/2)$ is the total
energy per unit volume, $e$ the specific internal energy, $g$ the
gravitational acceleration, $f_D$ the Darcy friction factor, and
$q_w''$ the wall heat flux into the fluid (per unit inner-surface area).
Wall shear appears only in the momentum equation: a stationary wall
performs no net work on the fluid, and frictional dissipation is
recovered automatically by the conservative total-energy formulation ---
no explicit dissipation source is required or permitted in
\cref{eq:energy}.

The one-dimensional (area-averaged) description assumes that radial
pressure and velocity nonuniformities are negligible, appropriate for
$L/D \approx 3600$; entrance and fitting losses are not represented
beyond the distributed friction term.

\subsection{Thermodynamic closure: the property interface}
\label{sec:interface}

The system is closed by an equation of state supplying
$(p, T, a) = f(\rho, e)$, where $a$ is the speed of sound. The flow
solver and the boundary conditions interact with the fluid
\emph{exclusively} through an abstract property interface comprising:
the closure $(\rho,e)\to(p,T,a)$ and its inverse $(p,T)\to(\rho,e)$;
the isentrope $\rho(p;\,p_{\mathrm{ref}},\rho_{\mathrm{ref}})$; the
outgoing-wave (Riemann-invariant) velocity relation
$u(p;\,p_{\mathrm{ref}},\rho_{\mathrm{ref}},u_{\mathrm{ref}})$; the
stagnation state from $(p,\rho,u)$; the isentropic-nozzle mass flux
$G(p_0,T_0,p_b)$; and, for fluids with a vapor dome, the saturation
margin $m(p,T) = T - T_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)$. This isolation is what
allowed the real-fluid \co\ implementation to replace the ideal-gas
closure without modification of the flow solver, and it is the seam at
which future two-phase closures will attach.

\subsection{Ideal-gas closure (nitrogen)}

For the nitrogen baseline the interface is implemented by a calorically
perfect gas,
\begin{equation}
p = \rho R T, \qquad e = c_v T, \qquad a = \sqrt{\gamma R T},
\end{equation}
with $R=\SI{296.8}{J/(kg.K)}$ and $\gamma=1.40$. The isentrope,
invariant, and orifice flux have their textbook closed forms
\citep{anderson1982}. The closure is accurate for nitrogen over the
conditions computed here except at the very end of deep adiabatic
blowdowns, where the fluid approaches the nitrogen critical temperature
(\SI{126.2}{K}) and real-gas corrections of several percent would
apply; this limitation is noted where relevant.

\subsection{Real-fluid CO$_2$ thermodynamics}
\label{sec:co2}

\subsubsection{Property model and validity range}

\co\ properties derive from the \citet{span1996} reference equation of
state --- a Helmholtz-energy formulation valid from the triple-point
temperature (\SI{216.59}{K}) to \SI{1100}{K} at pressures to
\SI{800}{MPa} --- evaluated through the open-source library CoolProp
\citep[version 8.0]{bell2014}. Because every quantity ($p$, $T$, $e$,
$h$, $s$, $c_p$, $c_v$, and the full thermodynamic speed of sound
$a^2 = (\partial p/\partial\rho)_s$) is obtained from derivatives of
the \emph{same} Helmholtz function, the property set is thermodynamically
self-consistent by construction; no auxiliary correlation for $a$ or
$c_p$ is introduced. Saturation properties
($T_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)$, $s_{\mathrm{vap}}(p)$) and, where the HEM choke
requires it, the vapor quality $x$ of flashed states come from the same
EOS via CoolProp's saturation routines and
pressure--entropy flash.

\subsubsection{Conversion of conservative variables to primitives}
\label{sec:tables}

The performance-critical operation is the per-cell closure
$(\rho, e) \to (p, T, a)$, executed for every cell at every time step
(and for every reconstructed face pair). Direct CoolProp flash calls at
this frequency are prohibitively slow (milliseconds per scalar call),
so the closure is served from precomputed tables on a uniform
$(\ln\rho, T)$ grid --- by default $512\times512$ nodes spanning
$\rho \in [0.5, 300]\,\si{kg/m^3}$, $T \in [220, 340]\,\si{K}$, far
inside the EOS validity envelope. The inversion proceeds in two steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is recovered from $(\ln\rho, e)$ by a vectorized Newton
iteration on the tabulated $e(\ln\rho, T)$, which is strictly monotone
in $T$ at fixed density for this fluid and range, so the iteration is
globally convergent from the mid-range initial guess (converged in
$\le 6$ iterations to $|\Delta T| < \SI{1e-6}{K}$);
\item $p$ and $a$ follow by bilinear evaluation at $(\ln\rho, T)$.
\end{enumerate}
Interpolation is the only consistency loss, and it is quantified by an
automated test that compares the closure against direct Span--Wagner
evaluation at random gas-phase states: maximum relative errors
$5\times10^{-5}$ ($p$), $1\times10^{-5}$ ($T$), and $6\times10^{-6}$
($a$); mean errors are an order of magnitude smaller. The closure costs
$\approx\SI{0.5}{ms}$ per 300-cell call. Table nodes that fall inside
the vapor dome are filled by gas-side extrapolation in $\ln\rho$ purely
so that dome-grazing lookups remain finite; \emph{model validity near
the dome is governed by the explicit saturation monitor of
\cref{sec:monitor}, never by those extrapolated nodes}.

\subsubsection{Numerical difficulties and safeguards near saturation}
\label{sec:safeguards}

Property inversion near the saturation boundary and the critical point
is the fragile part of any real-fluid solver, and the failures
encountered here are documented deliberately:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Sound speed is undefined inside the dome} in an equilibrium
sense, and CoolProp raises an exception when $a$ is requested of a
two-phase $(p,s)$ state. All isentrope evaluations used by boundary
physics therefore request only $(\rho, h)$ --- which equilibrium
flashes do provide --- and never $a$, through a dedicated low-level
\texttt{AbstractState} path.
\item \emph{Entropy--quality flashes proved non-robust} over parts of
the dome in the installed CoolProp version. The dome crossing of an
isentrope, $p_{\mathrm{dome}}(s)$, is therefore resolved from a
precomputed 200-node table of the saturated-vapor entropy
$s_{\mathrm{vap}}(p)$ (monotone; interpolated in $\ln p$), not from an
S--Q flash.
\item \emph{Riemann-invariant quadrature.} The outgoing-wave relation
$u(p) = u_{\mathrm{ref}} + \int_p^{p_{\mathrm{ref}}} dp'/(\rho a)$ is
integrated along the isentrope by 8-point Gauss--Legendre quadrature.
Both endpoints are clamped to the gas side of the dome crossing; if the
reference state itself grazes the dome (possible transiently at the
boundary cell before the monitor halts the run), the relation degrades
gracefully to the acoustic estimate
$u \approx u_{\mathrm{ref}} + (p_{\mathrm{ref}}-p)/(\rho_{\mathrm{ref}}
a_{\mathrm{ref}})$ instead of raising.
\item \emph{EOS validity floor at the choke.} During deep throat
expansion toward atmospheric back pressure late in a blowdown, the
throat isentrope can reach the Span--Wagner lower temperature limit
(\SI{216.6}{K}, the triple point) where the flash enters the
sublimation region and raises. The choke maximization of
\cref{sec:hem} treats any state the EOS refuses as ``invalid''
($G=0$) inside a bracketed scan, so the maximization is confined to
the valid region and the event is reported on a dedicated flag rather
than crashing the run (\cref{sec:hem-logic}).
\item \emph{Saturation line representation.} $T_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)$ is
interpolated from a 200-node table (verified interpolation error
$<\SI{0.01}{K}$); above the critical pressure
($p_c = \SI{7.377}{MPa}$) the margin gate uses the critical
temperature $T_c = \SI{304.13}{K}$.
\end{itemize}
These are failures (or fragilities) of the \emph{property evaluation},
and they are handled at the property layer; no instance required
modification of the fluid-dynamic solver, whose own failure modes
(positivity, CFL) are monitored independently.

\subsubsection{Single-phase validity monitoring}
\label{sec:monitor}

The distributed wellbore model is valid only for single-phase states.
The saturation margin $m_j = T_j - T_{\mathrm{sat}}(p_j)$ is evaluated
for \emph{every cell at every time step} (a vectorized table lookup).
If $\min_j m_j < 0$, the run stops immediately and reports the time,
location (cell center $z$, depth $\zeta$), and complete thermodynamic
state $(p, T, \rho, T_{\mathrm{sat}})$ of the first dome entry. The
margin minimum and the depth at which it occurs are recorded at every
history output, and the full field $m(z,t)$ is recoverable from the
$(p,T)$ snapshots. The single-phase model is never silently continued
through a two-phase state; flashing \emph{within the choke} is the one
sanctioned exception and is treated by the algebraic HEM boundary model
of \cref{sec:hem}, which leaves the distributed equations untouched.

\subsection{Wall friction}
\label{sec:friction}

When enabled (all campaign cases), the Darcy friction factor is
evaluated from the explicit all-regime correlation of
\citet{churchill1977},
\begin{equation}
f_D = 8\left[\left(\frac{8}{\mathrm{Re}}\right)^{12}
+ \frac{1}{(A+B)^{3/2}}\right]^{1/12},\quad
A = \left[2.457\ln\!\frac{1}{(7/\mathrm{Re})^{0.9}
+ 0.27\,\varepsilon/D}\right]^{16},\quad
B = \left(\frac{37530}{\mathrm{Re}}\right)^{16},
\label{eq:churchill}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{Re} = \rho |u| D/\mu$ and roughness
$\varepsilon = \SI{4.6e-5}{m}$ (commercial steel). Churchill's form was
selected over the implicit Colebrook equation because it is explicit
(no per-cell iteration), spans laminar, transitional, and turbulent
regimes continuously (important during flow reversal and near the
closed bottom where $\mathrm{Re}\to0$), and agrees with Colebrook
within a few percent in the fully turbulent range --- well inside the
uncertainty of applying any smooth-pipe-derived correlation to a real
casing. The viscosity is a constant reference value per fluid
($\mu = \SI{1.8e-5}{Pa.s}$ for N$_2$, \SI{1.5e-5}{Pa.s} for gaseous
\co); the sensitivity of the solution to $f_D$ is modest because the
discharge is choke-controlled, but friction is retained because it is
the physical damping mechanism of the acoustic reverberation
(\cref{sec:velocity}).

\subsection{Thermal boundary hierarchy}

The wall heat flux $q_w''$ is supplied by an interchangeable thermal
model: (1)~adiabatic wall, $q_w''=0$; (2)~prescribed wall temperature
with convective coupling; (3)~prescribed heat flux; and (4)~conjugate
coupling to transient radial conduction through the completion layers
and formation. The hierarchy, the preferred level, and the roles of the
lower levels are discussed with the boundary conditions in
\cref{sec:bc-thermal}; the conjugate model itself is formulated in
\cref{sec:thermal}.

\section{Initial conditions}
%=============================================================
\label{sec:ic}

\subsection{Initial temperature: uniform versus geothermal}
\label{sec:ic-temperature}

Two initial temperature distributions are implemented, both as plain
model inputs:
\begin{equation}
T(\zeta, 0) = T_0 \quad\text{(uniform)}, \qquad\text{or}\qquad
T(\zeta, 0) = T_s + \Gamma\,\zeta \quad\text{(geothermal)},
\label{eq:geo-ic}
\end{equation}
with $\zeta = L - z$ the depth below the wellhead. The uniform state is
retained as the conservative (coldest-margin) reference and for
continuity with the earlier nitrogen baseline. The geothermal form is
the physically motivated shut-in state of a well in thermal equilibrium
with its surroundings, and both parameters are adjustable inputs
(dataclass fields), not hard-coded constants.

For the Louisiana-summer nominal case:
\begin{itemize}
\item $T_s = \SI{305.4}{K}$ (\SI{32.2}{\celsius}, \SI{90}{\degree F}):
a representative warm summer-day wellhead temperature for Baton Rouge.
The NOAA 1991--2020 climate normals for Baton Rouge Metropolitan
Airport give a July daily mean of ${\approx}\,\SI{28.5}{\celsius}$ and
mean daily maximum of ${\approx}\,\SI{33}{\celsius}$ \citep{noaa2021};
\SI{90}{\degree F} sits between the two and corresponds to a shut-in
fluid column that has equilibrated during a hot afternoon.
\item $\Gamma = \SI{25.5}{K/km}$ (\SI{1.4}{\degree F}/100\,ft): a
typical Louisiana Gulf-Coast geothermal gradient; regional compilations
place coastal-Louisiana gradients in the range
${\approx}\,22$--$\SI{30}{K/km}$ \citep{blackwell2011}. The same value
has been the default of the formation thermal model since its
introduction, so fluid and formation are initialized from one profile.
\end{itemize}
\Cref{tab:geoT} tabulates the resulting initial fluid temperatures at
the wellhead, bottomhole, and the fiber-optic sensor depths of
\cref{sec:sensors}. Relative to the uniform \SI{300}{K} column, the
geothermal column is warmer everywhere and \emph{increasingly} so with
depth --- the property that matters for \co, because hydrostatic
compression raises $T_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)$ with depth: the uniform
column's saturation margin \emph{decreases} downhole (from \SI{12.6}{K}
at the wellhead to \SI{6.5}{K} at bottom), whereas the geothermal
gradient more than compensates, giving margins that \emph{increase}
from 32.4 to \SI{49.7}{\degree F} (18.0 to \SI{27.6}{K}) top to
bottom.

\paragraph{Documented approximation.} The seasonal temperature wave
penetrates only the top ${\sim}\SI{10}{m}$ of the ground, so
initializing the \emph{formation} with the summer surface temperature
slightly warms the shallow formation relative to the annual-mean
profile ($T_s = \SI{294.3}{K}$). For blowdown time scales
($\lesssim$ hours) the effect is confined to the near-wellhead cells
and is small against the \SI{18}{K} initial margin; both profiles
remain available as inputs, and the conjugate model accepts any
\texttt{Geothermal}$(T_s, \Gamma)$ instance.

\subsection{Initial pressure: hydrostatic initialization}
\label{sec:ic-pressure}

The initial pressure is \emph{not} uniform. For a \SI{609.6}{m} column
of \co\ at ${\sim}\SI{130}{kg/m^3}$, the gravitational head is
$\rho g L \approx \SI{0.77}{MPa}$ --- 15\,\% of the wellhead pressure
--- and even for nitrogen (${\sim}\SI{56}{kg/m^3}$) it is
\SI{0.33}{MPa}, 6.6\,\%. A uniform-pressure initial condition is
therefore not a quiescent state: it superposes a gravitational settling
transient of amplitude comparable to the valve-opening wave itself,
contaminating the early-time wave physics (this was observed directly
in the first $x$--$t$ diagrams of the project and is the reason
hydrostatic initialization was adopted). All campaign cases are
initialized hydrostatically with the wellhead pressure prescribed:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dp}{d\zeta} = \rho\!\left(p, T(\zeta,0)\right) g,
\qquad p(0) = p_{\mathrm{wh}},
\label{eq:hydro}
\end{equation}
integrated downward cell by cell with the midpoint rule
(half-cell step from the wellhead face to the first cell center, then
full-cell steps), evaluating $\rho(p,T)$ from the \emph{same} EOS used
by the solver, with the local initial temperature from
\cref{eq:geo-ic}. Pressure, temperature, and density are thus mutually
consistent by construction --- density is never imposed independently.
For the isothermal ideal gas the closed-form barometric profile
$p = p_{\mathrm{wh}}\exp\!\left[g\zeta/(RT_0)\right]$ is used instead
(and the numerical integrator reproduces it); for \co\ the ideal
formula is \emph{not} acceptable: at $Z \approx 0.69$ it underestimates
the pressure gradient by $1/Z - 1 \approx 45\,\%$, i.e.\ by
${\sim}\SI{0.35}{MPa}$ at bottomhole. The real-fluid hydrostatic bottom
pressure at \SI{5}{MPa}/geothermal is \SI{5.745}{MPa}
(\cref{tab:geoT}); nitrogen under the same conditions gives
\SI{5.331}{MPa}.

An admissibility consequence worth recording: a wellhead pressure of
\SI{6.5}{MPa} at \SI{300}{K} was found to be \emph{infeasible} as a
single-phase \co\ initial condition --- the hydrostatic column crosses
$p_{\mathrm{sat}}(\SI{300}{K}) = \SI{6.71}{MPa}$ at depth, i.e.\ the
initial condition itself is two-phase. The saturation monitor flags
this at $t=0$, which is the designed behavior.

\subsection{Verification: stationarity of the closed well}
\label{sec:stationarity}

A hydrostatically initialized column with the valve closed must remain
stationary; any numerically generated motion measures the discrete
imbalance between the pressure-gradient flux and the gravity source
(the scheme is not well-balanced by construction, so a small residual
is expected and must be quantified). \Cref{tab:static} reports the
maximum residual velocity magnitude and pressure drift after
\SI{600}{s} of closed-well integration --- roughly $10^5$ acoustic
time steps --- for both fluids, both initializations, and two grids.
Residual velocities are a few centimeters per second at the campaign
grid and converge at first order with refinement (the slope limiter
reduces to first order at the local extremum of the
pressure-gradient/gravity balance); the associated pressure drift is
at most a few kPa out of \SI{5}{MPa} ($<10^{-3}$ relative); mass is
conserved to machine precision. Against blowdown velocities of
$\mathcal{O}(1$--$10)\,\si{m/s}$ and the \SI{130}{kPa} leading
expansion wave, the spurious motion is smaller by two to three orders
of magnitude. This test doubles as the
verification of the gravity source-term treatment and of the
real-EOS/geothermal initialization algorithm of \cref{eq:hydro}: an
inconsistent initialization (e.g.\ barometric pressure with real-fluid
density) produces an immediate settling transient orders of magnitude
larger.

\section{Boundary conditions}
%=============================================================
\label{sec:bc}

\subsection{Closed bottom}
\label{sec:bc-bottom}

The closed bottom is an exact solid wall implemented at the
reconstruction level: the wall-face \emph{left} state is the mirror
image (in velocity) of the MUSCL-reconstructed interior \emph{right}
state at that face, so the Riemann problem at the wall face is exactly
symmetric and the HLLC mass and energy fluxes vanish identically ---
not merely to truncation order. This matters: an earlier ghost-cell
variant that mirrored \emph{cell} states (rather than reconstructed
face states) leaked mass at the wall at $\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})$ per
step through the gravity-modified predictor, which the present form
eliminates exactly. The momentum flux at the wall face reduces to the
wall pressure, as it must.

\subsection{Wellhead: coupling of the interior to the choke}
\label{sec:choke}

\subsubsection{Why the wellhead is not a pressure boundary}

Imposing the environmental pressure $p_b$ directly on the uppermost
cell would be wrong in kind, not merely in degree: it removes the
restriction from the problem, so the discharge rate is set by the pipe
itself (the open-pipe limit, here ${\sim}\SI{143}{kg/s}$ at the
baseline state) rather than by the choke
(${\sim}\SI{1.7}{kg/s}$) --- two orders of magnitude apart. The
restriction is the element that couples the internal wellbore dynamics
to the environment, meters the discharge, and determines whether the
flow is choked; it must therefore appear \emph{in} the boundary
condition. Conversely, while the flow through the choke is choked, the
interior solution is causally disconnected from $p_b$, and the
boundary condition must reproduce that property exactly --- a
fixed-pressure condition cannot.

\subsubsection{The two-relation closure}

The pipe-side face state $(\rho_f, u_f, p_f)$ at $z=L$ is required to
satisfy simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Compatibility with the interior} (supply side): the face
lies on the isentrope through the adjacent interior state
$(\rho_i, u_i, p_i)$ and on its outgoing $C^{+}$ characteristic,
\begin{equation}
u_f = u_i + \int_{p_f}^{p_i}\frac{dp'}{\rho a}\bigg|_{s=s_i},
\qquad \rho_f = \rho(p_f;\,s_i),
\label{eq:cplus}
\end{equation}
which for the ideal gas reduces to the familiar
$u_f = u_i + \tfrac{2}{\gamma-1}(a_i - a_f)$,
$\rho_f = \rho_i (p_f/p_i)^{1/\gamma}$;
\item \emph{Valve mass balance} (demand side): the flow accepted by
the choke, modeled as a quasi-steady nozzle fed by the stagnation
state $(h_{0f}, s_f)$ of the face flow, equals the pipe efflux:
\begin{equation}
\rho_f u_f A = C_d A_t\, G\!\left(p_{0f}, T_{0f}, p_b\right),
\qquad A_t = \pi d_c^2/4,
\label{eq:valve}
\end{equation}
with the nozzle flux $G$ of \cref{sec:hem}.
\end{enumerate}
The quasi-steady treatment of the choke is justified by scale
separation: the residence time in the restriction
($\sim d_c/a \approx \SI{e-4}{s}$) is four to five orders of magnitude
shorter than the discharge time scale, so the nozzle traverses a
sequence of steady states.

Substitution of \cref{eq:cplus} into \cref{eq:valve} yields a scalar
equation for $p_f$, solved by Brent iteration on a bracket bounded
below by the \emph{sonic face pressure} --- the $C^{+}$ coupling
forbids a supersonic pipe exit; if the valve demands more than the
sonic pipe supply, the exit itself runs sonic, recovering the
open-pipe choking limit as $A_t \to A$ --- and above by the pressure
at which \cref{eq:cplus} brings the face to rest. When the interior
stagnation pressure falls below $p_b$, the valve closes (check-valve
clamp; backflow is excluded by assumption). The resulting face flux
$[\rho_f u_f,\; \rho_f u_f^2 + p_f,\; u_f(E_f + p_f)]$ \emph{replaces}
the HLLC flux at the top face, so the interior update and the
discharge remain mutually consistent through the entire
choked~$\to$~unchoked~$\to$~closed sequence, and
$\mdot(t) = \rho_f u_f A$ is obtained as part of the solution rather
than prescribed.

\subsubsection{Real-fluid implementation: demand/supply splitting}
\label{sec:split}

For the real fluid, every evaluation of \cref{eq:cplus,eq:valve}
involves CoolProp flash calls, so the exact nonlinear solve at every
time step is costly. The implementation splits the two physical time
scales:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{demand} $C_d A_t G(p_0, T_0, p_b)$ depends on the
slowly evolving stagnation state and is recomputed only when the
wellhead pressure has drifted by 0.2\,\% since the last evaluation;
\item the \emph{supply} response \cref{eq:cplus} must track the
boundary cell \emph{every} step and is applied in linearized
(acoustic) form,
$u_f = u_i + (p_i - p_f)/(\rho_i a_i)$,
$\rho_f = \rho_i + (p_f - p_i)/a_i^2$, with the face internal energy
from the isentropic linearization
$e_f = e_i + (p_i/\rho_i^2)(\rho_f - \rho_i)$; the resulting quadratic
in $p_f$ is closed in four Newton steps. The linearization error is
$\mathcal{O}\!\left[(\Delta p_{\mathrm{face}}/\rho a^2)^2\right]
\approx 10^{-5}$ for choke-dominated discharge, where the face
pressure defect is small. The exact nonlinear solve (quadrature
invariant plus Brent) is retained in the code for verification and
for large-$\Delta p$ configurations such as the open-pipe limit.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Negative result (recorded deliberately).} The first
implementation amortized CoolProp cost by freezing the \emph{entire}
face flux over a 5-step stride. The frozen outflow decouples from the
boundary cell and the pair oscillates unstably, draining the wellhead
cell by ${\sim}\SI{2}{MPa}$ within a second of simulated time --- an
artifact that initially masqueraded as physical dome entry and was
exonerated only by running nitrogen through the identical general-EOS
code path. The lesson is structural: \emph{the fast-acoustic part of an
outflow boundary cannot be time-lagged}; only the slow (demand) part
can. With the split above the boundary is stable and the real-fluid
overhead is ${\sim}\SI{3}{ms}$ per step.

\subsection{The choke flux: single-phase and homogeneous-equilibrium
formulations}
\label{sec:hem}

\subsubsection{Physical problem}

High-pressure fluid from the wellbore enters the choke and expands
toward the back pressure. Two qualitatively distinct limits exist. If
the pressure ratio is modest, the flow exits subsonically and the
discharge responds to $p_b$ (\emph{subcritical}). If the ratio is
large --- the prevailing condition for most of a blowdown --- the flux
reaches a maximum at a sonic throat state and becomes independent of
$p_b$ (\emph{critical/choked}). For \co\ a third element enters:
depending on the upstream state, the expansion path may cross the
vapor--liquid saturation boundary \emph{inside} the restriction, so
that the throat state is a flashing two-phase mixture even while the
wellbore fluid upstream is entirely single phase. At the campaign
initial state (\SI{5}{MPa}, \SI{300}{K}) this is not an edge case: the
isentrope from the stagnation state reaches the dome \emph{before} the
sonic point, so the choke flashes from the first instant.

\subsubsection{Isentropic-nozzle flux}

The choke is modeled as an isentropic nozzle: the expansion through
the restriction is fast (residence $\sim\SI{e-4}{s}$) and compact, so
heat exchange and friction within it are neglected, and all
irreversibility is lumped into the discharge coefficient $C_d$. Along
the isentrope $s = s_0$ from the stagnation state $(h_0, s_0)$,
energy conservation gives the throat velocity
$w(p_t) = \sqrt{2\left[h_0 - h(p_t, s_0)\right]}$ and the mass flux
\begin{equation}
G(p_t) = \rho(p_t, s_0)\;\sqrt{2\left[h_0 - h(p_t, s_0)\right]}.
\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
The physical throat state maximizes $G$ over the throat pressure:
\begin{equation}
G^* = \max_{p_b \le p_t \le p_0} G(p_t), \qquad
\text{choked} \iff p_t^* > p_b,
\label{eq:Gmax}
\end{equation}
the maximizer being the sonic condition $w = a$ expressed variationally
--- a form that requires no sound-speed evaluation and therefore
remains meaningful for two-phase (equilibrium) throat states. If the
maximizer exceeds $p_b$ the flow is choked at $G^*$; otherwise the flux
is evaluated at $p_t = p_b$ (subcritical). For the calorically perfect
gas \cref{eq:G,eq:Gmax} reduce to the textbook result with critical
pressure ratio $\left(2/(\gamma+1)\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$, which
the implementation uses directly on the ideal path.

\subsubsection{Homogeneous Equilibrium Model for flashing in the choke}
\label{sec:hem-model}

When the isentrope crosses the saturated-vapor line at
$p_{\mathrm{dome}}(s_0)$ before the maximizer is reached, the throat
state is two-phase and \cref{eq:G} requires mixture properties. These
are supplied by the \emph{Homogeneous Equilibrium Model} (HEM): the
density and enthalpy in \cref{eq:G} are the equilibrium-flash values
from the same Span--Wagner EOS,
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{HEM}}^{-1} = \frac{1-x}{\rho_\ell(p_t)}
 + \frac{x}{\rho_v(p_t)},
\qquad
h_{\mathrm{HEM}} = (1-x)\,h_\ell(p_t) + x\,h_v(p_t),
\qquad
x = \frac{s_0 - s_\ell(p_t)}{s_v(p_t) - s_\ell(p_t)},
\label{eq:hem}
\end{equation}
evaluated in practice by CoolProp's $(p,s)$ equilibrium flash. The
assumptions, stated explicitly, are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{local thermodynamic equilibrium} between the phases at
every point of the expansion (no metastability, no superheated liquid
or subcooled vapor);
\item \emph{mechanical equilibrium}: both phases share one pressure;
\item \emph{thermal equilibrium}: both phases share one temperature,
the saturation temperature at the local pressure;
\item \emph{no slip}: equal phase velocities, so a single mixture
momentum/energy balance applies and \cref{eq:G} retains its
single-stream form;
\item \emph{instantaneous phase change} relative to the residence time
through the restriction --- nucleation and interphase transfer are
assumed fast on $\sim\SI{e-4}{s}$;
\item \emph{homogeneous mixture}: the two-phase medium is treated as a
single pseudo-fluid with the mixture properties \cref{eq:hem}.
\end{enumerate}
Assumption 5 is the physically weakest for short restrictions: real
flashing nozzles exhibit delayed nucleation and thermodynamic
non-equilibrium that \emph{raise} the critical flow above the HEM
prediction, which is why HEM is generally the conservative
(lower-bound) estimate for short orifices
\citep{starkman1964,henry1971}. Non-equilibrium corrections (e.g.\ the
Henry--Fauske model) and slip corrections exist, as does Leung's
$\omega$-method correlation of the HEM result \citep{leung1986}; they
are deliberately not adopted at this stage because (i)~HEM is the
standard, parameter-free first model, (ii)~the correction magnitude
depends on choke internals not yet fixed for the facility, and
(iii)~at the conditions computed here the throat barely enters the
dome (quality $x_t \ge 0.93$ in all campaign results), so the
difference between HEM and its corrections is small compared with the
$C_d$ uncertainty. HEM is appropriate as the first model precisely
because it confines all two-phase physics to an algebraic boundary
relation --- the distributed wellbore equations remain single-phase,
which is the intended staging of the project; a full two-phase
wellbore model is deferred, not approximated.

The practical consequence of the HEM extension is modest at the
campaign initial state and grows as the well cools: at
\SI{5}{MPa}/\SI{300}{K} the sonic point sits just inside the dome
(throat quality $x_t = 0.975$) and the HEM critical flux exceeds the
flux evaluated at the dome crossing (the previous single-phase bound)
by only 1.4\,\% --- i.e.\ the earlier dome-limited treatment was
already an excellent approximation there. The real-gas HEM flux
exceeds the ideal-gas formula by 17\,\% at the same state (the
$\sqrt{\rho_0 p_0}$ scaling at $Z = 0.69$), and reduces to it at low
pressure (\cref{sec:vv-choke}). In the time domain, the HEM choke
shortens the adiabatic uniform-IC \co\ run's time to in-wellbore dome
entry from \SI{193}{s} to \SI{173}{s} --- a direct, integrated measure
of the flux correction.

\subsubsection{Discharge coefficient and effective area}
\label{sec:cd}

The only empirical element of the choke model is the discharge
coefficient in the effective area $C_d A_t$: it lumps the
vena-contracta contraction, boundary-layer blockage, and
multidimensionality of the real restriction. The baseline value
$C_d = 0.84$ represents a rounded-entry choke-type restriction,
between the sharp-edged-orifice value ($C_d \approx 0.61$) and the
smooth converging nozzle ($C_d \approx 0.95$--$1.0$)
\citep{perry2008}; the facility hardware ultimately fixes it, and it
should be treated as the principal calibration parameter when
blowdown data become available. Its influence on the solution is
simple and was verified numerically: because the discharge is
choke-controlled and quasi-static, $\mdot \propto C_d$
instantaneously, and the blowdown clock stretches as $1/C_d$.
A sweep over $C_d \in \{0.61, 0.72, 0.84, 1.0\}$ (nitrogen,
\SI{5}{MPa}, \SI{1200}{s}, adiabatic) gives initial mass flow rates
proportional to $C_d$ to within 0.2\,\% and half-inventory times
obeying the $1/C_d$ scaling to within 0.02\,\% (the product
$C_d\,t_{1/2}$ is constant across the sweep: 680.4/576.5/494.2/
\SI{415.2}{s} at $C_d$ = 0.61/0.72/0.84/1.0) --- the adiabatic
choke-controlled blowdown is self-similar in the stretched time
$C_d t$, so the entire uncertainty in $C_d$ maps onto the blowdown
clock and nothing else. No vena-contracta pressure correction is applied
beyond $C_d$ itself, and the throat area is the geometric bore area
$A_t = \pi d_c^2/4$ with $d_c = \SI{12.7}{mm}$.

\subsubsection{Flow-regime classification and decision logic}
\label{sec:hem-logic}

The model distinguishes explicitly among five conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{single-phase subcritical discharge} --- maximizer of
\cref{eq:Gmax} at $p_t = p_b$, isentrope entirely outside the dome;
\item \emph{single-phase critical (choked) discharge} --- interior
maximizer, dry isentrope down to $p_t^*$;
\item \emph{flashing during the expansion} --- the isentrope crosses
$p_{\mathrm{dome}}(s_0)$ above the throat pressure, so part of the
expansion path is two-phase (flag \texttt{choke\_flashing});
\item \emph{two-phase critical discharge by HEM} --- the maximizer
itself lies inside the dome; the reported throat quality
\texttt{choke\_quality} is the HEM $x_t$ at $p_t^*$;
\item \emph{two-phase conditions upstream, inside the wellbore} ---
outside the validity of the present distributed model; the run is
\emph{halted and flagged} with the complete first-entry state
(\cref{sec:monitor}), never silently extrapolated.
\end{enumerate}
Conditions 1--4 are handled by the wellhead boundary model; condition
5 terminates the simulation by design. \Cref{fig:logic} gives the
per-time-step decision logic.

\subsection{Radial thermal boundary}
\label{sec:bc-thermal}

Four radial thermal boundary treatments are implemented, forming a
verification hierarchy:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{adiabatic}, $q_w'' = 0$: the fluid-dynamic reference; it
bounds the expansion cooling from below (coldest case) and is retained
as the limiting/sensitivity case, not as a physical model of the
facility;
\item \emph{prescribed wall temperature}, $q_w'' = h\,(T_w - T)$ with
$h$ from the Dittus--Boelter correlation (\cref{sec:correlations}):
bounds the heat ingress from above (the wall never cools) and provides
a closed-form check on the convective coupling;
\item \emph{prescribed heat flux}: completes the hierarchy (used in
code verification of the source term);
\item \emph{conjugate completion--formation coupling}
(\cref{sec:thermal}): the preferred physical model, in which
$q_w''$ \emph{emerges} from the coupled transient solution.
\end{enumerate}
The coupled formulation is preferred for a physical reason, not a
formal one: the wall heat flux during blowdown is controlled by the
transient depletion of heat stored in the casing, cement, and
near-well formation, with the air-filled annulus as the dominant
resistance (\cref{sec:results-thermal}); no prescribed-$T_w$ or
prescribed-flux model can represent that depletion a priori, because
the flux history depends on the very cooling it mitigates. Levels 1--3
are retained because they bracket the answer and isolate individual
couplings for verification.

\label{eq:network}
\end{equation}
with the fluid coupling at the innermost node through the series
conductance of the convective film and the inner half-shell,
$G_f' = \left[\,1/(2\pi r_1 h) + R'_{\mathrm{half}}\,\right]^{-1}$, and
the outer boundary held at the undisturbed geothermal temperature
$T_{\mathrm{geo}}(\zeta) = T_s + \Gamma\,\zeta$ --- the \emph{same}
profile used to initialize the fluid column in the geothermal cases, so
fluid, completion, and formation start in mutual equilibrium and the
initial wall heat flux is identically zero. For blowdown time scales
the rock thermal penetration depth $\sqrt{\alpha t}$ is of order
centimeters ($\alpha = k/\rho c_p \approx \SI{9e-7}{m^2/s}$,
$\sqrt{\alpha t} \approx \SI{6}{cm}$ at one hour), so the \SI{5}{m}
far-field radius is generous by two orders of magnitude and the
far-field boundary condition (fixed $T_{\mathrm{geo}}$) cannot
influence the solution on these time scales. The formation shells are
logarithmically spaced to resolve the near-wellbore gradient.

The wellbore/formation heat flux is computed as part of the coupled
solution rather than prescribed because it is the \emph{answer}, not
an input: during blowdown the flux is set by the competition between
the cooling rate of the fluid (which depends on the discharge) and the
depletion rate of heat stored in the completion (which depends on the
flux history). Any prescribed-flux or prescribed-temperature closure
presupposes that history.

\subsection{Empirical correlations: documentation}
\label{sec:correlations}

\paragraph{Fluid-side convection: Dittus--Boelter.} The convective
coefficient on the casing inner surface is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Nu} = \max\!\left(0.023\,\mathrm{Re}^{0.8}\mathrm{Pr}^{0.4},
\; 3.66\right),
\qquad h = \mathrm{Nu}\,k_{\mathrm{fluid}}/D,
\label{eq:db}
\end{equation}
the classical \citet{dittus1930} form (heating exponent $0.4$; see
\citet{winterton1998} for the equation's provenance), with a laminar
floor at the constant-wall-temperature fully developed value
$\mathrm{Nu} = 3.66$ so that $h$ remains physical as $u \to 0$
(closed well, end of blowdown). Nominal validity:
$\mathrm{Re} \gtrsim 10^4$, $0.7 \le \mathrm{Pr} \le 160$, fully
developed pipe flow, moderate property variation. During the campaign
discharges $\mathrm{Re}$ ranges from $\mathcal{O}(10^6)$ (early) down
through the transitional regime (late), so the correlation is
extrapolated through transition by the $\max(\cdot)$ switch ---
a documented approximation. Dittus--Boelter was selected over the more
accurate \citet{gnielinski1976} correlation (which covers
$3\times10^3 < \mathrm{Re} < 5\times10^6$ with friction-factor
coupling) because the fluid-side film is \emph{not} a controlling
resistance here: it carries ${\sim}\SI{3}{K}$ of the
${\sim}\SI{11}{K}$ total fluid-to-formation difference
(\cref{sec:results-thermal}), so the ${\sim}20\,\%$ accuracy
difference between the two correlations perturbs the heat flux by only
a few percent --- below the uncertainty of the air-annulus closure.
The Prandtl numbers and viscosities entering \cref{eq:db} are constant
gas-phase reference values ($\mathrm{Pr} = 0.72$/$0.78$,
$\mu = 1.8/1.5\times10^{-5}\,\si{Pa.s}$ for N$_2$/\co); the energy
equation itself never uses these transport constants.

\paragraph{Air annulus: Willhite's effective conductivity.} The
gas-filled 7-5/8$\times$13-3/8\,in annulus is treated as a massless
nonlinear conductance following the standard wellbore treatment of
\citet{willhite1967}: natural convection enters through an effective
conductivity from the Dropkin--Somerscales correlation
\citep{dropkin1965},
\begin{equation}
k_{\mathrm{eff}} = \max\!\left[\,k_{\mathrm{air}},\;
0.049\,(\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathrm{Pr})^{1/3}\,\mathrm{Pr}^{0.074}\,
k_{\mathrm{air}}\right],
\qquad
\mathrm{Gr} = \frac{g\,\beta\,\Delta T\,(r_o - r_i)^3}{\nu^2},
\label{eq:keff}
\end{equation}
in parallel with gray-surface radiation between concentric cylinders:
linearized conductance per unit length
$2\pi r_i\,\sigma F\,(T_i^2 + T_o^2)(T_i + T_o)$ with the two-surface
view factor
$F = \left[1/\varepsilon_i + (r_i/r_o)(1/\varepsilon_o - 1)
\right]^{-1}$ and $\varepsilon_i = \varepsilon_o = 0.8$ (oxidized
steel). The Dropkin--Somerscales correlation was developed for
enclosed natural convection at high Rayleigh number and is the closure
recommended by \citet{willhite1967} for casing annuli --- the
standard of wellbore thermal engineering since --- which is the basis
for its selection; its nominal range
($5\times10^4 < \mathrm{Gr\,Pr} < 7.17\times10^8$) comfortably
contains the annulus conditions here
($\mathrm{Gr\,Pr} \sim 10^6$--$10^7$ for $\Delta T \sim 1$--
\SI{10}{K} across a \SI{60}{mm} gap). The $1/3$-power makes
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ gap-width independent, which limits sensitivity to
the exact annular geometry. The conductance is re-evaluated every
time step from the current surface temperatures (coefficient
lagging), keeping the implicit radial solve linear. Because the
annulus is the dominant solid-side resistance
(\cref{sec:results-thermal}), this closure is the leading-order
empirical sensitivity of the entire thermal model: if the annulus
fluid, pressure, or emissivities of the facility differ from the
assumed air/0.8 values, this term --- not the formation conductivity
or the film coefficient --- is where the model should be recalibrated.

\paragraph{Formation conduction.} Pure transient conduction with
constant properties ($k = \SI{2.1}{W/(m.K)}$,
$\rho c_p = \SI{2.3e6}{J/(m^3.K)}$, representative of water-saturated
Gulf-Coast sediments); no empirical correlation is involved, and the
discretization is verified against an independent reference solution
(\cref{sec:vv-thermal}).

\subsection{Solution procedure and coupling}

The networks are advanced once per flow time step with backward Euler;
each axial cell requires one tridiagonal (Thomas) solve, vectorized
across all cells. The fluid temperature enters the solid solve
explicitly, while the heat flux returned to the wellbore energy
equation,
\begin{equation}
q_w'' = \frac{G_f'\,\left(T_1^{\,n+1} - T_f\right)}{2\pi r_1},
\end{equation}
uses the \emph{implicit} wall temperature through the same
conductance. The energy delivered by the fluid source term in
\cref{eq:energy} is therefore exactly the energy absorbed by the
network, and the global budget of \cref{eq:budget}, extended by the
wall-heat integral $\int \sum_j q_w''\,\pi D\,\Delta z\,dt$, continues
to close to machine precision in the conjugate case.

\section{Numerical method}
%=============================================================
\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Finite-volume discretization and HLLC flux}

Equations \eqref{eq:mass}--\eqref{eq:energy} are discretized on $N$
uniform cells of width $\Delta z = L/N$ in conservative finite-volume
form,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{V}_j^{n+1} = \mathbf{V}_j^{n}
 - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta z}\left(\mathbf{F}_{j+1/2} -
 \mathbf{F}_{j-1/2}\right) + \Delta t\, \mathbf{S}_j,
\end{equation}
with interface fluxes from the HLLC approximate Riemann solver
\citep{toro1994,toro2009}. HLLC restores the contact wave omitted by
HLL --- immaterial for the smooth blowdown interior but retained for
consistency with the shock-capturing benchmarks and for robustness in
later two-phase extensions. For the ideal gas the wave-speed estimates
are the standard pressure-based ones of \citet{toro2009}; for the
general EOS they are the direct bounds of
\citet{davis1988} and \citet{einfeldt1988},
$S_L = \min(u_L - a_L,\, u_R - a_R)$,
$S_R = \max(u_L + a_L,\, u_R + a_R)$, which require no auxiliary
thermodynamic evaluations; the HLLC star states are built from the
face triples $(\rho, u, p, e, a)$ without any EOS call inside the
flux, so the Riemann solver itself is EOS-agnostic.

\subsection{Reconstruction and predictor}

Second-order accuracy in space and time is obtained with the
MUSCL--Hancock scheme: primitive variables $(\rho, u, p)$ are
reconstructed with van~Leer-limited slopes \citep{vanleer1984}, and
the half-step predictor advances the face states with the primitive
form of the equations, the pressure predictor using the coefficient
$\rho a^2 = \rho\,(\partial p/\partial\rho)_s$ --- which equals
$\gamma p$ for the ideal gas and is its correct generalization for an
arbitrary EOS. Gravity is included in the velocity predictor
($-g\,\Delta t/2$), maintaining formal second order for the
gravitational dynamics.

For the real fluid, two additional primitives are reconstructed and
advanced: the internal energy $e$ (predictor
$\dot e = -u\,\partial_z e - (p/\rho)\,\partial_z u$) and the sound
speed $a$ (slope-limited, no predictor). This avoids per-face EOS
inversions entirely; the price is an $\mathcal{O}(\Delta z^2)$
thermodynamic mismatch of the independently reconstructed face triple
$(\rho, p, e)$, which is standard practice for real-gas MUSCL schemes
and converges away with the scheme's own truncation error.

\subsection{Source-term treatment and energy consistency}
\label{sec:sources}

The momentum sources (gravity, friction) and the energy source (wall
heat) are advanced with a Heun (trapezoidal) corrector with the wall
heat flux held fixed over the step (one thermal-network solve per
step). The gravitational \emph{work} term in \cref{eq:energy} requires
care: evaluating it as $-\rho_j u_j g$ at cell centers leaves a
residual in the discrete energy budget that does \emph{not} converge
with mesh or time-step refinement, because the potential-energy
inventory evolves according to the \emph{face} mass fluxes while the
work term samples cell-centered momenta. The term is therefore
discretized in the energy-consistent face-flux form
\begin{equation}
\left(-\rho u g\right)_j \;\longrightarrow\;
 -\,g\,\frac{F^{\rho}_{j-1/2} + F^{\rho}_{j+1/2}}{2},
\label{eq:gravwork}
\end{equation}
where $F^{\rho}$ denotes the time-centered HLLC mass flux. With
\cref{eq:gravwork} the discrete budget
\begin{equation}
\sum_j \left(E_j + \rho_j g z_j\right) A\,\Delta z
\;+\; \int_0^t \mdot\,\left(h_0 + gL\right)\,dt'
\;-\; \int_0^t\!\! \sum_j q_w''\,\pi D\,\Delta z\;dt'
\;=\; \text{const}
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
telescopes exactly (machine precision), where $h_0 = (E_f + p_f)/
\rho_f$ is the stagnation enthalpy of the effluent evaluated from the
face flux itself --- a bookkeeping identity exact for \emph{any} EOS.
This discovery path (cell-centered form, $\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})$
non-convergent residual, face-flux repair) is recorded because budget
closure is used throughout this report as a primary verification
instrument, and its integrity depends on this detail.

\subsection{Time stepping, caching, and stopping criteria}

Time integration is explicit under
$\Delta t = \mathrm{CFL}\,\Delta z / \max_j\left(|u_j| + a_j\right)$
with $\mathrm{CFL} = 0.8$ unless stated otherwise. For the ideal-gas
path the choke boundary may be recomputed on a stride (default 1); for
the real fluid the demand/supply split of \cref{sec:split} applies
and the supply side is per-step by construction. A run terminates on:
(i)~reaching $t_{\mathrm{end}}$; (ii)~in-wellbore dome entry
(\cref{sec:monitor}), with the flagged report; or (iii)~a positivity
failure of $(\rho, p)$, which raises immediately --- no positivity
``fixes'' are applied, so a failure of the fluid-dynamic solver is
visible as such rather than masked (none occurred in any run reported
here). Grid resolution is $N = 300$ for the hour-long campaign cases
and $N = 600$ for the short wave-resolution runs; the grid study of
\cref{sec:vv-solution} quantifies the residual discretization error.

\subsection{Summary of modifications for the real fluid}

Moving from ideal-gas nitrogen to real-fluid \co\ changed the solver
in exactly five places, all behind the property interface:
(i)~$(\rho,e)\to(p,T,a)$ served by the tabulated closure
(\cref{sec:tables}); (ii)~reconstruction of $e$ and $a$ as additional
primitives with the $\rho a^2$ pressure predictor; (iii)~Davis/Einfeldt
wave speeds in the HLLC flux; (iv)~the choke demand from the real-gas
HEM maximization with the demand/supply split (\cref{sec:split});
(v)~the per-step saturation monitor. The source terms, the bottom
boundary, the thermal coupling, and the budget machinery required no
modification --- the budget identity $h_0 = (E_f+p_f)/\rho_f$ holds
for any EOS.

\paragraph{Conservation audits.} For every discharging case reported
in this document --- both fluids, all thermal models, all
initializations --- the mass balance
$M(t) + \int_0^t \mdot\,dt' = M(0)$ and the energy balance
\cref{eq:budget} close to relative errors of
$\mathcal{O}(10^{-16}$--$10^{-12})$ (the individual values are listed
with the campaign summary, \cref{tab:cases}). Acceptance: $<10^{-12}$.
This is the test that exposed the cell-centered gravitational-work
defect (\cref{sec:sources}) and the ghost-cell wall leak
(\cref{sec:bc-bottom}) during development.

\paragraph{Hydrostatic stationarity.} \Cref{sec:stationarity} and
\cref{tab:static}: residual velocities of $\mathcal{O}(\si{cm/s})$,
first-order convergent, and pressure drift $<10^{-3}$ relative over
\SI{600}{s} of closed-well integration, for both EOS types and both
temperature profiles.

\paragraph{Thermal-network verification.} Four tests establish the
radial model: (i)~\emph{steady state}: with constant fluid and
far-field temperatures the converged heat flow is identical across
every radial interface to $10^{-6}$ relative (series-resistance
consistency); (ii)~\emph{energy conservation}: with an insulated
outer boundary, heat absorbed from the fluid equals the change of
stored network energy to $10^{-10}$ (backward Euler is exactly
conservative); (iii)~\emph{transient conduction}: a formation-only
network driven by a constant wall temperature reproduces the
constant-temperature-cylinder solution to better than
0.5\,\% over dimensionless times
$\tau = \alpha t/r_w^2 \in [10, 1000]$, the reference being an
independent Crank--Nicolson solution on a 6000-node logarithmic
radial grid; (iv)~\emph{radial grid convergence} of $q_w''(t)$:
$<2\,\%$ change from 16 to 32 formation shells.
\label{sec:vv-thermal}

A methodological finding is recorded explicitly: three convenient
references were tried first for test (iii) and all proved inadequate
--- the leading-order logarithmic asymptote is 8--15\,\% in error over
this $\tau$ range; Ramey-type dimensionless-temperature correlations
\citep{ramey1962,hasan1991} address the constant-\emph{flux} boundary
condition rather than constant temperature; and naive quadrature of
the exact Carslaw--Jaeger integral \citep{carslaw1959} mishandles the
singular $u \to 0$ region of the integrand that dominates at large
$\tau$. The network initially appeared 3--7\,\% ``in error'' against
these references; the independent finite-difference solution showed
the network to be accurate to 0.3\,\% and the references to be at
fault. The hardcoded reference constants now carry their provenance
in the test source.

\paragraph{Real-fluid property closure.} \Cref{sec:tables}: tabulated
$(\rho,e)\to(p,T,a)$ closure versus direct Span--Wagner evaluation at
200 random gas-phase states --- maximum relative errors
$5\times10^{-5}$ ($p$), $1\times10^{-5}$ ($T$), $6\times10^{-6}$
($a$); acceptance $10^{-3}$. Thermodynamic consistency of the sound
speed: tabulated $a$ versus finite differences of $(\partial
p/\partial\rho)_s$ along CoolProp isentropes at three states ---
agreement within 0.2\,\%.

\subsection{Solution verification}
\label{sec:vv-solution}

\paragraph{Axial grid.} For the \SI{13.79}{MPa} nitrogen baseline over
the first \SI{60}{s}, the maximum relative deviation of $\mdot(t)$
from an 800-cell reference is 2.6\,\% (100 cells), 0.88\,\% (200), and
0.26\,\% (400); the campaign grid of $N=300$ sits between the latter
two. The early-wave acoustic structures of \cref{sec:velocity} were
additionally confirmed grid-converged by comparing peak perturbation
velocities at 200/400/1200 cells (1.18/1.20/\SI{1.21}{m/s}).

\paragraph{Time step.} Reducing CFL from 0.9 to 0.5 and 0.2 changes
$\mdot(t)$ by at most 0.6\,\% and 1.2\,\% respectively (the slight
increase at small CFL reflects accumulated first-order source-split
error over more steps, not instability).

\paragraph{Choke demand caching.} The 0.2\,\% demand-refresh
tolerance of \cref{sec:split} was verified against per-step demand
recomputation on a short run: $\mdot(t)$ differences below 0.05\,\%.

\subsection{Validation and model benchmarks}
\label{sec:vv-model}

\paragraph{Quasi-static lumped-vessel limit.} With gravity
deactivated, the 1-D solution must approach the zero-dimensional
adiabatic-vessel model (isentropic residual gas, quasi-steady orifice
discharge) whenever the acoustic transit time $L/a_0 \approx
\SI{1.7}{s}$ is small against the discharge time scale
($\approx\SI{200}{s}$, baseline choke). \Cref{fig:vessel}: maximum
relative deviations 0.9\,\% ($p$) and 0.8\,\% ($\mdot$) after the
initial wave transient. With larger chokes the \emph{wellhead}
pressure sits several percent below the column mean --- a genuine 1-D
effect of sustained expansion toward the choke --- while the mass
inventory continues to track the lumped model to 0.5\,\%. The
vessel comparison is run on the \emph{mass inventory}, not the
wellhead pressure, precisely because the latter legitimately deviates.

\subsection{Baseline: adiabatic nitrogen blowdown at 13.79 MPa}
\label{sec:results-n2}

The original nitrogen baseline (uniform \SI{300}{K}, hydrostatic,
\SI{13.79}{MPa}, adiabatic, $D = \SI{0.1}{m}$) is retained unchanged.
\Cref{fig:xt} shows the $x$--$t$ diagram of the pressure perturbation
during the first \SI{8}{s}: opening the choke launches an expansion
wave that descends at the sound speed, reaches bottom at
$t = L/a_0 \approx \SI{1.7}{s}$, reflects, and returns at
$2L/a_0 \approx \SI{3.4}{s}$; because the choke passes only a small
fraction of the sonic pipe flux, the wellhead acts as a nearly closed
end and the wave re-reflects as an expansion, producing a staircase
depressurization of period $2L/a_0$ with steps of
${\approx}\SI{19}{psi}$ (\SI{130}{kPa}). The initial wave is weak
relative to the inventory ($\rho a\,\Delta u \sim \SI{22}{psi}$
against \SI{2000}{psi}) and the subsequent discharge is quasi-static,
consistent with the lumped-vessel agreement of \cref{sec:vv-model}.
\Cref{fig:profiles,fig:histories} show the field evolution: the
column depressurizes nearly uniformly, the velocity profile is linear
in depth --- the signature of spatially uniform expansion --- with
superposed decaying acoustic oscillations, and expansion work cools
the inventory to \SI{-231}{\degree F} (\SI{127}{K}) by \SI{600}{s},
essentially the nitrogen
critical temperature; the ideal-gas closure is thus increasingly
strained exactly where the adiabatic blowdown ends, one motivation
for the real-fluid stage.

\subsection{Thermal-model hierarchy}
\label{sec:results-thermal}

\Cref{fig:hierarchy} compares the thermal levels on the actual well
geometry (bore ID \SI{170.3}{mm}, 12.7\,mm choke, N$_2$,
\SI{13.79}{MPa}) over \SI{1800}{s}. The outcome is not a small
correction: heat ingress from the completion and formation nearly
suppresses the expansion cooling --- minimum wellhead temperature
\SI{50.8}{\degree F} (\SI{283.6}{K}, coupled) and
\SI{62.7}{\degree F} (\SI{290.2}{K}, prescribed $T_w$) against
\SI{-236.5}{\degree F} (\SI{124.0}{K}, adiabatic). The warmer inventory sustains a higher
pressure at equal mass, so the heated cases also discharge measurably
faster. \Cref{fig:radial} locates the controlling resistances: of the
total fluid-to-formation temperature difference, roughly \SI{3}{K}
falls across the convective film, \SI{7}{K} across the air annulus,
and under \SI{1}{K} across all remaining solids; the formation beyond
the cement is essentially undisturbed after \SI{30}{min}. Two
consequences: the air-annulus closure \cref{eq:keff} is the
leading-order empirical sensitivity of the thermal model
(\cref{sec:correlations}); and the near-isothermal outcome is
specific to slow (choke-controlled, $\sim$tens of minutes)
discharge --- larger chokes shorten the residence time relative to
the thermal response and shift the behavior back toward the adiabatic
limit.

\subsection{Matched N$_2$/CO$_2$ campaign, uniform 300 K
initialization}
\label{sec:results-stage1}

\Cref{fig:cmp-hist} compares both fluids at matched conditions
(\SI{5}{MPa} wellhead, uniform \SI{300}{K}, hydrostatic, LSU
geometry, 12.7\,mm choke, $N=300$, \SI{3600}{s}), each with coupled
and adiabatic walls. The principal quantitative contrasts, with their
causes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Inventory}: \SI{4551}{lbm} (\SI{2064}{kg}) of \co\
against \SI{1779}{lbm} (\SI{807}{kg}) of N$_2$ --- a factor 2.56,
the product of the molar-mass ratio
(1.57) and the compressibility loading $1/Z$ at $Z = 0.687$ (1.46,
with a small hydrostatic correction). Real-gas attraction packs
substantially more \co\ into the same volume than any ideal estimate.
\item \emph{Initial discharge}: \SI{3.8}{lbm/s} (\SI{1.71}{kg/s},
\co) against \SI{2.7}{lbm/s} (\SI{1.22}{kg/s}, N$_2$), factor 1.40
--- the critical flux scales as
$\sqrt{\rho_0 p_0}$, so the density advantage enters only under a
square root, and the real-gas enhancement (1.17 over ideal \co)
contributes the rest.
\item \emph{Blowdown duration}: the ratio of inventory to discharge
rate, $2.56/1.40 \approx 1.8$, stretches the \co\ blowdown clock
accordingly (visible in the $M/M_0$ panel); both wells still reach
${\sim}2\,\%$ inventory within the hour.
\item \emph{Cooling}: with the coupled wall, minimum wellhead
temperatures are \SI{59.0}{\degree F} (\SI{288.1}{K}, \co) and
\SI{61.2}{\degree F} (\SI{289.4}{K}, N$_2$) ---
formation heat ingress nearly cancels the expansion cooling for both
--- but the heat drawn differs strongly: \SI{137.8}{MJ} (\co) against
\SI{65.2}{MJ} (N$_2$), reflecting the larger mass and the higher
$c_p$ (${\sim}\SI{1803}{}$ vs ${\sim}\SI{1039}{J/(kg.K)}$ at the
initial state). Adiabatically, N$_2$ cools freely to
\SI{-285.6}{\degree F} (\SI{96.7}{K}), while \co\ never gets the
chance (next item).
\item \emph{Single-phase validity}: the coupled \co\ case remains
single phase throughout, but barely --- minimum saturation margin
\SI{5.1}{\degree F} (\SI{2.84}{K}), at the \emph{bottom} of the
well. The adiabatic \co\ case is halted by the monitor at
$t = \SI{173}{s}$ (HEM choke; 193~s with the earlier
single-phase-bound choke) with first dome entry at the bottom
($\zeta = \SI{1997}{ft}$; \SI{705}{psi} / \SI{55.7}{\degree F},
i.e.\ \SI{4.863}{MPa} / \SI{286.3}{K}). The bottom location is the
hydrostatic-margin effect of \cref{sec:ic-temperature}: compression
raises $T_{\mathrm{sat}}$ downhole, so the uniform column's thinnest
margin is at depth and bulk expansion cooling consumes it first.
\item \emph{Choke flashing}: the uniform-IC \co\ choke is in HEM
regimes 3--4 from the first instant: throat quality $0.95$--$1.0$ for
the first \SI{9.6}{min} of the coupled run (then the throat dries),
and for the entirety of the (shorter) adiabatic run
(\cref{fig:choke-hem}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Approach evaluation: HEM choke and geothermal
initialization}
\label{sec:results-stage2}

Two measures against saturation proximity were evaluated at full
resolution, separately and in combination: the HEM choke
(\cref{sec:hem-model}) and the geothermal summer initialization
(\cref{sec:ic-temperature}). \Cref{fig:margin-zt} maps the saturation
margin field $m(z,t)$ for the four \co\ cases;
\cref{fig:min-margin} tracks its minimum and the depth at which the
minimum occurs; \cref{fig:sat-paths} shows the corresponding $p$--$T$
trajectories against the saturation curve; \cref{fig:choke-hem} shows
the HEM choke behavior. The outcome:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Uniform \SI{300}{K}, coupled}: single phase for the full
hour with minimum margin \SI{5.1}{\degree F} (\SI{2.84}{K}) at the
bottom at $t = \SI{482}{s}$; the choke flashes for the first
\SI{9.6}{min}.
Workable, but the margin is thin and entirely owed to formation heat
ingress.
\item \emph{Uniform \SI{300}{K}, adiabatic}: dome entry at
\SI{173}{s} at the bottom. Flagged and halted.
\item \emph{Geothermal, coupled} --- the preferred configuration:
single phase throughout with minimum margin \SI{25.6}{\degree F}
(\SI{14.2}{K}; $t = \SI{809}{s}$, near bottom), wellhead temperature
never below \SI{79.4}{\degree F} (\SI{299.5}{K}), and the choke
\emph{never} flashes (the warmer stagnation states keep the nozzle
sonic point dry). Formation heat ingress \SI{109.1}{MJ}.
\item \emph{Geothermal, adiabatic}: dome entry at \SI{503}{s} --- at
the \emph{wellhead} this time (\SI{402}{psi} / \SI{16.8}{\degree F},
i.e.\ \SI{2.77}{MPa} / \SI{264.7}{K}). The
geothermal gradient removes the weak point at the bottom, so
sustained expansion cooling wins first where the initial temperature
is lowest. The initialization delays first entry by a factor 2.9 but
cannot prevent it: without heat ingress the cooling is unbounded, so
\emph{every} adiabatic \co\ blowdown of this well ends in the dome
eventually.
\end{enumerate}
\Cref{fig:cmp-hist-geo} shows the corresponding N$_2$/\co\ histories
at the geothermal initialization.
The margin maps make the mechanism legible: the critical window is
the first 10--20 minutes; once the depressurization transient has
passed and the discharge rate has fallen, heat ingress dominates and
margins grow beyond \SI{72}{\degree F} (\SI{40}{K}) everywhere. The engineering summary is
that \emph{the coupled thermal path plus a summer-warm initial column
keeps the entire system --- wellbore and choke throat --- comfortably
single phase for this choke size}; the HEM choke extension is the
necessary model element only for cold initial columns (and, by its
construction, it degrades gracefully to the single-phase model
whenever the throat is dry).

\subsection{Wave propagation: N$_2$ versus CO$_2$}

\Cref{fig:wave} compares the bottomhole arrival of the valve-opening
expansion wave at the geothermal initialization (600 cells). The
\co\ wave arrives at ${\sim}\SI{2.7}{s}$ against ${\sim}\SI{1.7}{s}$
for N$_2$: the \co\ sound speed at the initial state
(${\approx}\SI{732}{ft/s}$, \SI{223}{m/s}) is depressed well below

and its own ideal-gas estimate
($\sqrt{\gamma R T} \approx \SI{886}{ft/s}$, \SI{270}{m/s}) by

the same non-ideality that raises the density. The reverberation
period and the staircase structure scale accordingly.

\subsection{Fiber-optic sensor outputs}
\label{sec:sensors}

The LSU well instrumentation motivates a dedicated output layer:
distributed temperature sensing (DTS) along the bore and a fiber on
the outside of the 13-3/8\,in casing (cf.\ the optic-fiber runs in
\cref{fig:well-schematic,fig:well-completion}). The model samples
\emph{virtual sensors} at the corresponding locations: depths
$\zeta \in \{50, 500, 1000, 1500, 1950\}\,\si{ft}$, at two
\emph{radial} stations --- in the fluid (bore DTS) and on the
13-3/8\,in casing outer surface (external fiber), the latter
available in conjugate runs only, since only the coupled model
carries radial temperature information. The axial/radial distinction
is kept explicit throughout: a ``sensor'' is a (depth, radial
station) pair, and the radial station determines which physics it
sees.

\Cref{fig:sensors-geo} shows the full sensor set for the headline
(geothermal, coupled, HEM) \co\ case;
\cref{fig:profiles-geo} the corresponding $T(\zeta)$ and $p(\zeta)$
profiles at selected times with the sensor depths marked; and
\cref{fig:cmp-sensors} the N$_2$/\co\ bore-DTS comparison at two
depths (uniform IC). Three observations carry over from the nitrogen
phase and sharpen for \co: (i)~the bore DTS tracks the full transient
with excursions of a few \si{\degree F} in coupled cases; (ii)~the
external 13-3/8\,in fiber is nearly isolated from the transient by
the air annulus --- excursions below \SI{1}{\degree F}
(\SI{0.5}{K}) in \SI{30}{min} --- so it
measures the completion's slow thermal depletion, not the fluid
transient; a measurable deviation on that fiber would indicate an
annulus condition different from the air-filled assumption (e.g.\
liquid in the annulus), making it a useful diagnostic of annulus
integrity; (iii)~depth resolution matters mainly through the
geothermal offset and the hydrostatic margin effect, the transient
shape being nearly depth-uniform in the quasi-static regime.
Animations of the evolving fields with the sensor markers, and the
complete numerical sensor histories
(CSV: per-sensor $T(t)$ with location metadata; fluid $p, \rho, u$ at
the bore stations; field profiles at selected times), accompany the
repository for direct comparison with experimental records; the
report figures are selected static views of the same data.

\subsection{Velocity fields: diagnosis of the oscillatory structure}
\label{sec:velocity}

The velocity field is the least smooth of the reported quantities and
is presented without smoothing, per the project's standing rule that
numerical results are diagnosed, not cosmetically filtered.
\Cref{fig:velocity} separates the two regimes for both fluids. During
the first seconds (left panels) the profile is the superposition of
the growing linear expansion profile and the descending/reverberating
wave fronts --- sharp but grid-converged structures (peak
perturbation velocities 3.87/3.94/\SI{3.97}{ft/s}, i.e.\
1.18/1.20/\SI{1.21}{m/s}, at 200/400/1200 cells in the nitrogen
study), i.e.\ \emph{physics}, not oscillatory error: the staircase
reverberation of \cref{fig:xt} seen in velocity. At late times
(right panels) the profile is linear in depth ---
spatially uniform expansion --- with small residual acoustic ripples
that decay on the frictional time scale: with the Churchill friction
model active the late-time ripple amplitude is damped by roughly two
orders of magnitude relative to a frictionless run, identifying
wall friction (a physical mechanism, not numerical dissipation) as
the damping agent. The \co\ panels show the same structure on a
slower acoustic clock and with smaller velocities --- the denser
fluid needs less velocity for the same mass flux.

\subsection{Long-time behavior}

By the end of the hour both coupled cases approach a slow, nearly
isothermal terminal regime: the discharge rate has fallen below
$\mathcal{O}(0.05)\,\si{kg/s}$, the choke is approaching (N$_2$) or
has passed (late \co) the choked/unchoked transition, the column
temperature relaxes back toward the geothermal profile on the
completion's thermal time scale, and the remaining inventory
(${\sim}2\,\%$) drains at near-ambient driving pressure until the
check-valve condition closes the boundary. The model's terminal state
is thus thermal re-equilibration with the formation at ambient
backpressure --- the physically expected end state.

\section{Conclusions and outlook}
%=============================================================
\label{sec:outlook}

A conservative Godunov/HLLC finite-volume model of vertical-well
blowdown has been implemented and verified through the real-fluid
\co\ stage. The documented chain is: a property interface implemented
by an ideal gas and by the Span--Wagner EOS with a quantified table
closure; hydrostatic initialization consistent with a geothermal
temperature profile, verified by closed-well stationarity; a wellhead
boundary that couples the interior characteristic to a quasi-steady
choke, extended to a homogeneous-equilibrium formulation that admits
flashing within the restriction while the wellbore remains strictly
single phase under an every-step saturation monitor; and a conjugate
completion--formation thermal model with every empirical correlation
documented, verified against analytical limits and an independent
transient-conduction reference. Budgets close to machine precision in
every reported run.

The physical findings: (i)~at matched \SI{5}{MPa}/\SI{300}{K}
conditions \co\ stores $2.56\times$ the nitrogen mass but discharges
only $1.40\times$ faster, with a correspondingly longer blowdown and
a slower acoustic clock ($a \approx 732$ vs \SI{1158}{ft/s}, i.e.\
223 vs \SI{353}{m/s});
(ii)~formation heat ingress, not fluid inertia, is what keeps the
cooling \co\ out of the vapor dome --- adiabatically the dome is
reached in minutes regardless of initialization; (iii)~the
geothermal summer initialization converts a marginal single-phase
case (\SI{5.1}{\degree F} / \SI{2.8}{K} minimum margin, flashing
choke) into a comfortable one (\SI{25.6}{\degree F} / \SI{14.2}{K},
dry choke), and relocates the eventual adiabatic failure point from
bottomhole to wellhead; (iv)~the HEM choke
correction is small where the throat barely enters the dome
($+1.4\,\%$ at the initial state) but is the element that makes the
cold-column cases well-posed at all; (v)~the external 13-3/8\,in
fiber is nearly isolated by the air annulus and thus functions as an
annulus-integrity diagnostic rather than a transient sensor.

The deferred items are explicit: two-phase conditions \emph{inside
the wellbore} halt the simulation by design and await a distributed
two-phase model (the framework's two-phase shock-tube heritage is the
intended starting point); non-equilibrium and slip corrections to the
HEM choke become relevant once facility hardware fixes the restriction
geometry and data allow $C_d$ calibration; the tubing, injection
line, and gauge carriers inside the 7-5/8\,in casing are not yet
represented; and fluid-side transport properties are constants, to be
revisited for transcritical operation.
